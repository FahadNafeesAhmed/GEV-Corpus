_AI-assisted research input for the team, disclosed as such under the competition rules. Not submission text. Every figure carries a source so a teammate can re-check it._

# How elite stock-pitch winners think: a playbook

## 1. What this is based on, and how far to trust it

This playbook comes from 20 case studies.

- **17 contest winners or placers:**
  - 5 from the Pershing Square Challenge (Columbia MBA)
  - 4 CFA Research Challenge global champions
  - 5 from undergraduate contests (Wharton WITG, Balyasny, Cornell, Michigan)
  - 3 from Sohn idea contests
- **3 famous professional pitches:** Ackman on Canadian Pacific (long) and on Herbalife (short), and Einhorn on Green Mountain Coffee (short).

Eleven are longs and nine are shorts.

Most cases were read from the teams' own decks, memos and recorded pitches. No winning Excel model was available to read. So what this playbook says about model structure is rebuilt from what the teams showed in their decks and memos.

Keep three limits in mind.

- **We mostly see winners.** Losing finalists rarely publish. A trait found in every winner may also be found in every loser.
- **Winning is not the same as being right.** Fifteen cases have a price about 12 months after the pitch. In 8 the stock moved the way the team said, and in 7 it did not. Only one, the Vital Farms short, was clearly right inside 12 months for the reason it gave. Canadian Pacific worked through its stated mechanism, but over three years. Judges score the process. The market pays for something else.
- **No past HFAC x Citadel winning memo is public.** Several searches found none. That supports "not public" but does not prove it. Citadel publishes three criteria: quality of the thesis, how well the model matches the thesis, and the logic of the model's assumptions. Everything else about what these judges want is inferred from how similar buy-side judges behaved.

Each principle below carries a label:
- **Strong:** many cases and few counterexamples.
- **Moderate:** several cases, with real counterexamples.
- **Weak:** few cases, or inference.

## 2. The intuition in six ideas

**1. A pitch is a disagreement with a price. (strong)** Winners reduce the thesis to one number. It sits on one model line, for one named year, printed next to the Street's number. "The Street", or "consensus", means the average sell-side analyst forecast. A useful test is this sentence: "the market expects X (source), the price implies Y, we expect Z". If you cannot fill it in, you have a description, not a pitch. Winners often listed four to eight drivers, but in every case one number carried the value.

**2. The proof is usually arithmetic, not a secret. (strong)** In 10 of 20 cases, the proof that carried the variance was simple math on public data. Teams turned a price, a forecast or a guidance figure into a physical quantity. Examples:
- implied load factor, meaning the share of an airline's seats that are filled
- implied share of households
- employers still left to win as clients
- stores per million people

Then they checked that quantity against its own history or a hard ceiling. A judge can check a multiplication in seconds. A judge cannot check a single growth rate.

**3. Price equals earnings times the multiple. Over 3-12 months, the multiple usually decides. (strong)** The multiple is what investors pay for each dollar of profit. Two common versions:
- **P/E:** price divided by earnings per share.
- **EV/EBITDA:** enterprise value divided by EBITDA. Enterprise value (EV) is the value of the shares plus net debt. EBITDA is operating profit before depreciation and amortization.

Most winners that lost money were right about the business and wrong about the multiple or the timing. The Deere short saw earnings fall further than it forecast, yet the stock rose because the market paid a recovery multiple. In the Carlisle 2025 and Baker Hughes 2026 cases, the business delivered and the stock still fell.

**4. Research serves one driver. (moderate)** Primary research means calls, surveys and site visits. It appeared in about 13-14 of 20 cases, but it carried the variance in only 2. Usually it confirmed one link in a chain that arithmetic or careful reading of company filings had already built. Big call counts impress people. They are not the wedge.

**5. The risk you call "noise" is often the one that hurts you. (moderate)** Qantas treated its reputation trouble as media noise. A regulator's lawsuit and the CEO's early exit then wiped out the 12-month gain. DoorDash left courier legal risk out of its memo. A judge raised it, and a courier-pay settlement later hit the stock.

**6. Judges reward a process they can audit. (strong)** Every published rubric scores reasoning, evidence and how well the model fits the thesis. None can score accuracy, because results arrive after the prize. So build the pitch to survive a skeptical reader, not to sound confident.

## 3. The procedure, with gates

Each step ends in a gate: evidence you must have before you move on.

### Step 1. Check that the stock fits the format

Pershing teams chose their stock for the contest before they had a thesis. The DoorDash team rejected most of a few dozen names in about ten minutes each. They kept names that passed three filters:
- a business a judge understands at once
- a lopsided payoff: much more to gain than to lose
- main debates that research can settle in the time available

Most chosen names also had a visible reason for doubt: a price drop, a short report, a guidance cut, or heavy short interest. Short interest is the share of stock sold short by investors betting on a fall.

When the stock is assigned, as in the CFA challenge, or already chosen, the edge moves elsewhere. It moves to finding the one debated variable inside the company.

**Gate:** you have three things:
- a one-line business description
- the recent price move and its stated cause
- two or three research actions that could settle the main debate

If you cannot name those actions, the stock does not fit the format. **(moderate)** The CFA champions had no choice of stock and still won.

### Step 2. Write down what the market believes, in numbers

The best pitches pinned down the other side before building their own view.
- **Blue Bird** opened with what the short sellers believed. It sized their view two ways: by short interest, and by the margin cut the shorts expected. The judge praised exactly that.
- **Einhorn** restated the sell side's $9 earnings-per-share model line by line, with its source, before changing anything.
- **Lamb Weston's Sohn deck** set the prevailing view against the variant view in one table.

**Gate:** you can fill in the sentence "market believes X (source), price implies Y, we believe Z", and X and Y are numbers. **(strong)** Two CFA champions skipped this step. They won on thoroughness, found no mispricing, and lost money over 12 months.

### Step 3. Turn the price into one physical number

Six cases ran a reverse DCF. A DCF, or discounted cash flow, values a stock from the cash it will produce in future. A reverse DCF starts from today's price and solves for the one operating number that price must assume.
- **Qantas:** the price implied a load factor below any year on record.
- **Freshpet:** the price needed 94% of management's own household market by 2034.
- **Vital Farms:** the target needed about 54% market share, and that share had just fallen from about 84%.

Then test rival explanations. The Qantas team was right that the implied load factor was too low. But the earnings beat came from higher fares, and load factor came in below the team's own forecast. A different variable explained the same price.

**Gate:** one implied quantity, compared with its own history or a physical limit, plus a note on which other variables could explain the price. **(moderate)** Many winners never used this tool. It is still the variant view a judge can check most easily.

### Step 4. Break the headline into drivers, pick one to four, and concede the rest

Winners multiplied the headline number out into its parts. Examples:
- users × orders per month × basket size
- capacity ÷ demand = utilization, then profit per pound
- brewers installed × pods used per day × profit per pod
- seats × load factor × fare

Then they fought only on the inputs they had evidence for.
- **Einhorn** accepted the bulls' market size. He fought only on usage, private-label competition and profit per pod.
- **The Dollarama team** said only one to four things move the value. Those became the rows of its scenario table.

**Gate:** three things:
- a driver tree that reaches the model's lines
- for each driver, your number next to consensus for a named year
- a written list of the drivers where you accept consensus

A top-down plug fails this gate. Motorola's CFA team simply cut costs by 2% a year, and actual margins missed its forecast by 4-5 points. **(strong)**

### Step 5. Measure how far you are from consensus, and whether that is enough

Where the gap was disclosed, student winners mostly sat 7-22% away from Street earnings or revenue in the key year. Professional shorts went further, at 30-61%. A few winners sat close to consensus in the near term: Valvoline and three CFA teams.

Then run the ratio test. For longs, the estimate gaps were small, from a few percent up to about 16%. The targets were large, from +22% to +95%. The rest of the return had to come from a higher multiple or from the passage of time. A Pershing judge asked the Baker Hughes team why a 16% beat should produce 63% upside. The honest answer is that it is a multiple call. A multiple call needs its own evidence and its own trigger.

**Gate:** a bridge that splits the target return into three parts:
- the estimate change
- the multiple change
- the roll-forward, meaning the value gained just by moving a year closer

If most of the return comes from the multiple, write down the evidence for the multiple. **(strong)** The size of the gap did not predict outcomes. The direction of the multiple did.

### Step 6. Aim research at the contested driver, and label the rest

Winners organized research by driver, not by topic.
- **DoorDash:** the team listed each segment's key issue and the research that would settle it, then made about 90 calls.
- **Valvoline:** the team wrote its own scraper for the one driver under test, which was room for new stores.
- **Vital Farms:** the team made about 100 calls to store managers on one question.

Two stopping rules appeared:
- **Stop when mock audiences stop questioning a pillar** (Valvoline).
- **Label research that moves nothing.** Lightspeed's CFA team marked its Glassdoor study as having no effect on valuation.

The best defense of a non-consensus number used three layers:
1. The company's own numbers, rearranged. This is the hardest to dismiss.
2. An outside anchor used as a ceiling or a floor: a foreign peer, a past cycle, or a best-in-class competitor.
3. Primary research that closes one link.

**Gate:** at least two independent sources for each contested input. Every finding either changes an input or is labelled as context. **(moderate)** Three winners did almost no primary research: Vertiv, Freshpet and Vestas. Two of them leaned on a reverse DCF instead. The third won a format that rewards thoroughness.

### Step 7. Audit the other side's method, and list the risks you are dismissing

The rebuttals that worked were method audits.
- **Dollarama:** the team showed the short seller had compared Canada with one US chain. The right comparison was the whole US market times Dollarama's share.
- **Canadian Pacific:** Ackman used management's own figure of 200-300 basis points to cap how much of the cost gap was truly "structural". (100 basis points = 1 percentage point.)
- **Einhorn** kept the bulls' model and changed only the inputs he had evidence for.
- **Blue Bird** conceded the lost subsidy. It then showed the loss fell on buses that earned almost no gross profit.

Two warnings:
- **Being right on the point you rebut did not save the trade.** Einhorn's earnings call was right, and the position still lost money.
- **Rebutting an argument is a variant view only if the market's price reflects that argument.** Proving a lone outlier wrong can simply put you on consensus's side.

**Gate:** four things:
- the opponent's case, in their own numbers
- the one claim that matters most, with its method audited
- what you concede
- a written list of the risks you are dismissing, each with the observation that would prove it is not noise

**(moderate)**

### Step 8. Treat backlogs and contracts as a flow

A backlog is signed orders not yet delivered. Three winners were backlog businesses. None of them modelled the backlog turning into revenue year by year, and all three theses broke on that point.
- **Vestas** carried fixed-price orders into cost inflation. Gross margin fell below 1% in 2022.
- **Baker Hughes** booked record orders, and revenue stayed flat.
- **Motorola's** backlog was never modelled at all.

Two related cases broke on timing:
- **Lamb Weston's** multi-year contracts repriced more slowly than the thesis needed.
- **ASML's** orders arrived 18-24 months late.

**Gate:** a chain from backlog, to conversion by year, to revenue. It carries the margin locked into each year's orders and the contract terms on cost changes, such as escalators and pass-through clauses. **(moderate)** No winner did this, so the evidence comes from failures. A team that does it well goes beyond the set.

### Step 9. Value it, give the bear case a lower multiple, and make the arithmetic tie out

Memo-style winners (Pershing, Sohn) valued the stock on a multiple of next year's or later earnings. CFA teams led with a DCF. A 2018 invitation to an Alta Fox x HFAC competition advised against a DCF; that is one source, so treat it as weak. A reasonable reading: use a multiple as the main method, and a DCF or reverse DCF as a cross-check.

Four checks separated sturdy valuations from fragile ones.

1. **The multiple fits the thesis.** Freshpet's short kept a 28x growth multiple while arguing that growth would break. The Deere short needed about 10x trough earnings, against a history of 17x.
2. **The bear case moves the multiple.** The price broke through the stated bear case in 9 of the 13 cases that had one. The bear cases that held used low multiples drawn from history. Anchor your bear case on the stock's own past drawdowns and trough multiples. A drawdown is a fall from peak to low.
3. **The arithmetic ties out.** 14 of 20 cases had at least one bridge or consistency problem.
   - Cornell's Vertiv team added up future cash flows without discounting them.
   - The Sohn RealReal team appears to have treated net cash as debt.
   Judges caught neither error. Citadel explicitly scores the logic of assumptions, so assume its judges might look.
4. **Cash is real.**
   - Count stock-based pay (SBC) as a cost, and write "after SBC".
   - Include cash spent on acquisitions and buybacks.
   - Separate what the business does from what management chooses. Valvoline's thesis needed about $1.8 billion of buybacks. Management bought a company instead.

No winner weighted its scenarios by probability. Monte Carlo simulations appeared three times, all in CFA reports, and all gave false comfort because their inputs came from calm years. Vestas put its bear case at 6% odds, and the stock fell below it within a year.

**Gate:** four things:
- a bridge from EV to equity value to value per share
- a scenario table that flexes the same drivers and the multiple
- a two-way sensitivity of the one number against the multiple
- a checks tab

**(strong)**

### Step 10. Fix the horizon, the proof date and the kill rule, then decide direction

Wedges resolved fastest when frequent outside data fed a line the company reports. Examples: weekly egg prices for Vital Farms, and Nielsen scanner data for Freshpet.

They stalled when they waited on a third party's decision:
- the FTC for Herbalife
- EPA funding for Blue Bird
- an investor day with no stated pass mark for ASML

Match the valuation date to the judging horizon. Targets built on numbers four or more years out went flat or lost money over 12 months, even when the business delivered.

Only 3 of 20 cases stated a kill rule: a measurable condition under which the thesis is wrong. Lamb Weston's rule was to exit if profit per unit rose while new capacity came online. Following it would have saved the trade.

No case documented how it chose long versus short. Shorts leaned on one of two things: a temporary condition that the market was projecting forward, or filings that hid the true economics. Longs leaned on a temporary drag or on a business the market had misread.

**Gate:** three things:
- a number the company itself reports, on a known date inside the horizon, with a pass or fail level set in advance
- a written kill rule
- for shorts: cash, buybacks, takeover value and squeeze risk

**(moderate)**

### Step 11. Prepare Q&A as a deliverable, and update in the open

- Dollarama prepared 30+ questions and met no surprises.
- Valvoline ran six or seven mock pitches.
- CFA champions built roughly 100-155 appendix slides.

When the price moved before the final, Freshpet added a slide on what had changed since submission and restated the remaining gap.

**Gate:** a question bank aimed at the judge questions that recur (Section 6), plus one backup page for each likely attack. **(strong)**

## 4. What a model behind a 2-page memo looks like

This is inferred from memos and decks, because no winning Excel file was available.

1. **Summary:** price and date, 12-month target, bear/base/bull values, and the one number next to consensus.
2. **Inputs:** every assumption in one place, with a source column and a column for why you differ from the Street.
3. **Build:** units × price by segment, or backlog → conversion → revenue, with segment margins.
4. **Condensed income statement and free cash flow:** after SBC, including acquisition and buyback cash.
5. **Consensus comparison:** by year, for the one or two lines the thesis changes.
6. **Valuation:** a multiple justified by the stock's own history and its peers, an explicit EV → equity → per-share bridge, and a reverse DCF.
7. **Scenarios:** built on the same drivers plus the multiple, with the bear case anchored on past drawdowns.
8. **Sensitivity:** the one number against the multiple.
9. **Checks:**
   - present values add up
   - one share count and one debt figure are used everywhere
   - signs are right
   - the scenario base case equals the summary
   - every number in the memo equals the model

The rows of the memo's scenario table should be the model's input cells. That is the plainest way to show the model matches the thesis.

## 5. How winners write the memo

The Pershing 2-page memos follow one order:
1. The recommendation, with a target and the annual return it implies.
2. A one-paragraph business description.
3. Three or four numbered theses.
4. Valuation, with bear, base and bull cases.
5. Risks, with mitigants.

The first lines state the one number.

Fundamental Edge reviewed the Wharton memos. Its advice was to put three things together at the top: the thesis, the multiple times the metric, and the gap to the Street. Pershing judges told teams to show a multi-year stock chart early.

Keep method detail, such as scrapers and backtests, in an appendix or a model tab you can open during Q&A. Do not squeeze the 20-page CFA report format into two pages.

## 6. What judges ask

The questions repeat across contests:
- What in guidance or consensus has to change, and when? (the first finals question to Vital Farms)
- Your beat is 16%; why is the upside 63%? (Baker Hughes)
- How is SBC treated? The word "ex-SBC" flipped the meaning for DoorDash.
- Cash, buybacks and receivables (Progyny)
- Legal and labour exposure (DoorDash)
- Management's choices and franchise economics (Valvoline, Dollarama)
- Show your method. Did you use AI? (Valvoline 2024)
- Big holders selling stock, and input costs (Blue Bird)

Answers that pulled out prepared evidence worked. Answers that hurt:
- reframing the question
- saying the risk could not be quantified
- promising to get back to the judge

The risks that judges raised often showed up in the stock later.

## 7. Data, machine learning and AI

**No winner in the set used AI, large language models or machine learning to do its research. Or none said so. (strong)** AI appeared only as a thesis topic, in DoorDash, ASML, Vertiv and Lightspeed. It never appeared as a research tool.
- The one AI disclosure found was from the 2025 CFA champion. It said AI was used only for proofreading.
- There was one direct test. A Pershing judge asked the Valvoline team whether they had used AI. They answered by describing their own work: an R scraper, a Google Maps join and Census data.

Absence of disclosure does not prove non-use.

**Winners' data work was plain.** Typical moves:
- Scrape a public but scattered source.
- Join it to a public count, such as vehicle registrations, Census data or company filings, and count.
- Code complaints by type.
- Buy transaction data and compare it with a control group.
- Multiply headcount × role mix × market salary to restate a margin.

**Data earns its place when it sets one number the model depends on. (strong)** Five cases used data to drive a pillar:
- Herbalife's retail discount
- RealReal's effective gross margin
- Valvoline's floor on room for new stores
- Lamb Weston's utilization path
- Progyny's 465 employers left to win

Large counts that fed no model line were decoration. Examples: a sentiment score, a Glassdoor study, and simulations that just restated their inputs.

**Data proved the state of the business better than it proved timing. (moderate)** The five data-driven cases mostly lost money within their horizon. The data that lined up with the payoff ran ahead of a reported line and was tied to a known reporting date. That point is weak, from few cases.

**How to present data so it reads as rigour:**
- Name the claim the data tests.
- Report the result as one number next to consensus.
- Give the sample, dates, filters and a control group in one footnote.
- State the known bias and which way it cuts.
- Keep the method where you can open it in Q&A.
- Check that every figure from a dataset appears the same way everywhere.

**The rules differ by contest.**
- **HFAC x Citadel** allows AI for initial research only if you disclose it. It bans generated text in any part of the submission. Grammar checking is allowed.
- **The CFA challenge** allows AI with documented disclosure.

There is almost no record of how judges react to disclosed AI research. Under rules like these, the proof of human work is arithmetic, code and data joins the team did itself and can redo live. **(weak; inferred from the rules plus one Q&A)**

## 8. Failure modes to check against

1. The multiple was left undefended or held fixed (Deere, Freshpet, ASML, Valvoline).
2. A third party controlled the catalyst (Herbalife, Blue Bird, ASML).
3. The backlog was treated as protection, not as a flow with costs (Vestas, Baker Hughes, Motorola).
4. Management's choices were never stress-tested (Valvoline, Freshpet, Baker Hughes).
5. A risk dismissed as noise became the risk that killed the trade (Qantas, DoorDash).
6. Arithmetic errors flipped the headline (Vertiv, RealReal, Motorola).
7. A long-dated thesis was sold on a short horizon (Lightspeed, Vestas, ASML).
8. The key input was already public. ASML's key figure came from Micron's own earnings call. Dollarama's store runway came from management's own deck.
9. The short's path was never sized. Before the thesis paid, the stock rose against the short: RealReal +57% in a squeeze, Lamb Weston +38%, Herbalife +115%.

## 9. What winners share, and what merely appears

**Shared by nearly all winners:**
- a recommendation and target stated early
- a driver tree that reaches model lines
- the other side stated in numbers (13-14 of 20)
- one to four named drivers
- scenarios with values (13 of 20)
- at least one objection answered in advance

**Present, but seldom decisive:** big call counts, scraping, Monte Carlo, regressions, ESG sections, and a DCF as the main method.

**Rare or absent, and worth adding:**
- kill rules (3 of 20)
- backlog conversion by year (0)
- a bear case built on a lower multiple (a few)
- probability weights (0; untested with judges)
- AI tools (0)

**The honest summary:** a strong process gets you into the final. It does not predict the stock. The things that most often decided the 12-month result were the multiple, the timing, a dismissed risk and management's choices. Winners handled exactly those things worst. Doing them well is where a team can go beyond the winners studied.

# Lens: Wedge anatomy: which wedge types the 20 cases actually used, how far they sat from consensus, how each variance was prov

# Wedge anatomy across 20 pitch cases

## Scope and caveats

There are 20 cases. 17 are competition placings:
- Pershing Square Challenge: 5
- CFA Institute Research Challenge (CFA RC): 4
- Undergraduate contests: 5
- Sohn idea contests: 3

The other 3 are professional public pitches: pro-ackman-2012-cp, pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr and pro-ackman-2012-hlf. Across all 20 there are 11 longs and 9 shorts.

Evidence quality:
- sohn-ny-2019-lw scores 5.
- Fifteen cases score 4.
- Four score 3: psc-2022-pgny, witg-2025-vitl, witg-2025-blbd and sohn-ny-2024-asml.
- No case scores 1 or 2. I use the score-3 cases only next to stronger ones.

Key terms:
- A **wedge** is the specific thing you believe that the market does not.
- **Consensus** is the average sell-side forecast.
- A **multiple** is price divided by earnings (P/E), or enterprise value divided by EBITDA (EV/EBITDA).
- A **reverse DCF** solves for what the current price already assumes.

On HFAC x Citadel: five separate discovery searches found no public past winning memo. That supports the "not public" assumption. It does not prove it.

## 1. The wedge types winners actually used

I gave each case one primary engine, meaning the thing the variance finally rested on. 17 of the 20 cases also stack a second type (Table 1).

- **Wrong peer frame (misclassification): 4 primary, 9 appearances in total.** "The market files this company in the wrong bucket." Cases: psc-2026-bkr (oilfield services vs energy infrastructure), psc-2024-vvv, cfa-rc-2021-vws ("tech company") and cornell-ug-2024-vrt.
- **Temporary condition priced as permanent: 4 primary.** cornell? No: michigan-uic-2023-de (peak farm cycle), sohn-ny-2019-lw (capacity wave), witg-2025-vitl (bird-flu price floor) and psc-2026-dash (a one-time rebuild cost extrapolated into later years).
- **Hidden economics rebuilt from disclosures: 3 primary, 7 appearances.** sohn-london-2019-real (costs buried in opex), pro-ackman-2012-hlf (payout labels) and cfa-rc-2026-lspd (payments margin mix).
- **A wrong input inside consensus or a named model: 3 primary.** sohn-ny-2024-asml (HBM memory wafer intensity 3x, not 2x), pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr (re-ran the SunTrust $9 EPS model) and witg-2025-blbd (electric-bus gross margin near 5%).
- **Price-implied quantity versus history or a ceiling: 2 primary, 5 appearances.** cfa-rc-2023-qan: the price implied load factors below any year on record. bam-2025-frpt: the price implied 94% of management's own addressable market.
- **Bottom-up market or runway rebuild: 2 primary, 5 appearances.** psc-2022-pgny (465 employers left to win) and psc-2019-dol (2,437 stores, not 1,700).
- **Best-peer operating gap plus a change agent: 1.** pro-ackman-2012-cp.
- **No variant view: 1.** cfa-rc-2022-msi. Its present value was below the price, and its final target sat below the Street's.

Misclassification is the most frequent type, but as a primary engine it did worst. Only 1 of 4 worked on price (cornell-ug-2024-vrt), and that one rose because of the AI label it argued against.

## 2. Distance from consensus

Where the key-year gap is disclosed, the student winners mostly sat **7–22% away from Street earnings or revenue**:
- witg-2025-blbd: revenue +7%, EBITDA margin +150bp
- psc-2026-dash: EBITDA about +16%
- psc-2026-bkr: 2028 EPS +16.5%
- michigan-uic-2023-de: FY24 EPS −13%
- bam-2025-frpt: NTM EBITDA about −13% (my derivation)
- psc-2022-pgny: gross profit −22%

Four winners sat much closer to consensus:
- psc-2024-vvv: 0.6–5.7% on every line
- cfa-rc-2023-qan: under 1 point of margin, and its target was *below* the mean broker target
- cfa-rc-2026-lspd: near-term revenue *above* consensus
- cfa-rc-2021-vws: its 2021 numbers equalled guidance

The professionals went further: sohn-ny-2019-lw at −30% EBITDA and pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr at −61% long-run EPS.

Price targets:
- Twelve-month targets ran 15–48%.
- Multi-year targets ran 63–97% (psc-2026-dash, psc-2026-bkr, psc-2024-vvv, psc-2019-dol, pro-ackman-2012-cp).

Almost every case put its variance on revenue and margin. In at least eight cases, though, most of the return needed a change in the multiple. The clearest are psc-2026-bkr, psc-2024-vvv, cfa-rc-2021-vws, witg-2025-blbd and michigan-uic-2023-de.

**Is there a sweet spot?** Not in outcomes. Table 2 sets the ratio of target return to earnings gap against results. High ratios both worked (witg-2025-vitl, cornell-ug-2024-vrt) and failed (witg-2025-blbd, cfa-rc-2021-vws). Low ratios both worked (bam-2025-frpt) and failed (michigan-uic-2023-de).

bam-2025-frpt and michigan-uic-2023-de mirror each other:
- FRPT held the multiple fixed. The stock fell further because the multiple compressed.
- DE needed the multiple to compress to about 10x. It expanded instead, and the short lost even though EPS fell more than the team forecast.

So the direction of the multiple, driven by rates, sector moves and narrative, decided the outcome more often than the size of the gap.

Judges do check the ratio. A Pershing judge asked psc-2026-bkr why a 16% beat should produce 63% upside. What survived questioning was 10–20% on one named line in one named year, with a 12-month target close to that gap times a modest multiple change.

## 3. How the variance was proved

Table 3 classifies the evidence in each case. **Arithmetic on public data** was the most common *load-bearing* proof, in 10 cases. It means turning a price, a guide or a consensus number into a physical quantity and testing it:
- cfa-rc-2023-qan: implied load factor
- bam-2025-frpt: implied penetration
- witg-2025-vitl: required market share
- psc-2022-pgny: employer count
- psc-2019-dol: stores per million people times market share
- pro-ackman-2012-cp: operating ratio (costs as a share of revenue) after adjusting for haul length
- pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr: K-cups per brewer
- psc-2026-dash: users × frequency × basket
- cfa-rc-2026-lspd: new locations per sales rep
- sohn-ny-2024-asml: wafers per bit

Other load-bearing proof types:
- Disclosure reading: 5 cases
- Industry structure: 5
- Government or alternative data: 4
- Primary research: 2 (psc-2026-dash with about 90 calls; witg-2025-blbd with one competitor executive)

Primary research is **present in 13 of 20 cases but carries the variance in only 2.** Usually it confirmed one link in the chain:
- psc-2024-vvv: the scraped white-space map answered a question that was not the binding one.
- cfa-rc-2026-lspd: labelled its Glassdoor work "no impact on valuation".
- michigan-uic-2023-de: its five calls added colour.

## 4. "Why hasn't the market seen this?"

The answers fall into six groups (Table 4). The credible ones name a **structural information gap that a reader can check**:
- witg-2025-vitl: management switched its KPI from volume share to dollar share.
- pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr: the company stopped disclosing K-cups per brewer.
- psc-2026-dash: the company discloses no segment P&L.
- bam-2025-frpt: the main competitor is private, and Freshpet is the only public pure play.
- sohn-london-2019-real: the market-size figure came from a study the company commissioned for its IPO.

The weak ones are generic:
- "underappreciated" (cfa-rc-2021-vws)
- "markets obsess about the near term" (sohn-ny-2024-asml, where the key 3x input had come from Micron's own earnings call)
- "legacy shareholder base" with no holder data (psc-2026-bkr)

Three cases rested on facts that were already public:
- psc-2019-dol: management's own deck already called Canada underpenetrated.
- sohn-ny-2024-asml: the 3x HBM figure came from Micron's own earnings call.
- cornell-ug-2024-vrt: its demand case borrowed sell-side forecasts.

A strong answer still did not guarantee the mechanism played out. The gap in psc-2022-pgny (retention untested because of 3-year contracts) was real, but it took 27 months to show up.

## 5. Tests that separate a real wedge from a story

Each test is drawn from the cases.

1. **Line-item test.** Does the wedge change a named model line against a named consensus number for a named year? cfa-rc-2026-lspd and psc-2026-dash pass. cfa-rc-2021-vws and cfa-rc-2022-msi fail.
2. **Ratio test.** If the return is many times the estimate gap, you are making a multiple call, and it needs its own evidence and trigger. psc-2026-bkr, psc-2024-vvv and witg-2025-blbd had neither.
3. **Reveal-date test.** Which company-reported number, on which date, proves it? The fastest resolutions came from high-frequency outside data tied to reported lines: egg prices in witg-2025-vitl and Nielsen scanner data in bam-2025-frpt. The slowest waited on third parties: the FTC in pro-ackman-2012-hlf, EPA funding in witg-2025-blbd, and an investor day with no stated pass mark in sohn-ny-2024-asml.
4. **Rival-explanation test.** Could another variable explain the same price? The one-variable reverse DCF in cfa-rc-2023-qan got the load-factor floor right. But the earnings beat came from higher fares, while load factor came in *below* its forecast.
5. **Cash test.** Does the wedge move EBIT or cash? pro-ackman-2012-hlf's own slide shows EBIT unchanged. Every earnings print then argued against it.
6. **Counter-move test.** What can management or customers do?
   - bam-2025-frpt: management cut capex to protect margins.
   - psc-2024-vvv: management swapped buybacks for an acquisition.
   - pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr: Keurig 2.0 re-locked the pod ecosystem.

Arithmetic errors also created some of the apparent asymmetry:
- cornell-ug-2024-vrt added up free cash flows without discounting them.
- sohn-london-2019-real appears to have treated net cash as debt.

## 6. Rebuttal wedges

Nine cases were framed against a named report or a dominant narrative (Table 5). Two styles fared differently.

**Method audits** were right on the specific point they attacked:
- psc-2019-dol showed Spruce Point compared Canada with a single US chain instead of the whole market times Dollarama's share.
- pro-ackman-2012-cp adjusted for haul length and put a ceiling on management's "structural" excuse.
- pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr kept the SunTrust model and changed only the inputs it had evidence for.
- witg-2025-blbd accepted the policy hit, then showed it landed on low-margin electric buses.

**Narrative dismissals** often turned out to be the kill risk:
- cfa-rc-2023-qan: the "Joe Aston effect" (media noise) was followed by an ACCC lawsuit and the CEO's early exit, which erased the 12-month gain.
- psc-2026-dash: courier classification was left out of the memo's risks, and a courier-pay settlement then hit the stock.
- psc-2024-vvv: EV risk was carefully bounded, but EV was not what moved the stock.

Being right on the rebutted point did not save the trade in pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr. The EPS leg was right, but the core moat thesis failed.

## 7. What winners share versus what merely appears in winners

**Shared, 16 or more of 20:**
- The other side is stated in numbers.
- The thesis reduces to one to four drivers.
- There is a downside or bear case.

**Present but rarely decisive:**
- Primary research counts
- Scraping (psc-2024-vvv, cfa-rc-2026-lspd)
- Monte Carlo simulations (cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2022-msi, cfa-rc-2023-qan)
- ESG sections

Winning is not being right:
- Only 2 of 20 cases were right on price *through the stated mechanism* within the horizon: witg-2025-vitl and pro-ackman-2012-cp.
- Another four were right on price for different reasons: psc-2019-dol, cornell-ug-2024-vrt, cfa-rc-2026-lspd and sohn-ny-2019-lw.
- Judges reward process. Stocks followed rates, sector re-ratings and shocks.

# Numbers, assumptions and model construction across 20 winning pitches

## Scope and caveats

The corpus has 20 cases:
- 5 Pershing Square Challenge (psc-*)
- 4 CFA Research Challenge (cfa-rc-*)
- 5 undergraduate contests (witg-2025-vitl, witg-2025-blbd, bam-2025-frpt, cornell-ug-2024-vrt, michigan-uic-2023-de)
- 3 Sohn contest winners (sohn-*)
- 3 professional benchmarks (pro-*)

Evidence quality: one case scores 5 (sohn-ny-2019-lw), fifteen score 4, and four score 3 (psc-2022-pgny, witg-2025-vitl, witg-2025-blbd, sohn-ny-2024-asml).

The biggest caveat is that I did not open a single winning Excel file. The DASH model sits behind a login, and the others were never published. So every claim about model structure is rebuilt from decks, memos and talks. Treat the tab-level advice at the end as inference, not observation.

## 1. Which models were actually built

**Revenue builds.** 13 of 20 built revenue as units × price or usage. Examples: users × orders per month × basket size (psc-2026-dash); stores × same-store sales (psc-2019-dol); capacity → utilization → profit per pound (sohn-ny-2019-lw); households × revenue per household (bam-2025-frpt); installed brewers × pods per day (pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr).

The rest took other routes:
- Three used segment growth rates only (psc-2026-bkr, cornell-ug-2024-vrt, michigan-uic-2023-de).
- One regressed segment revenue on 17 macro series with about 9 data points (cfa-rc-2022-msi).
- One used an engineering demand framework (sohn-ny-2024-asml), one a forensic reclassification (pro-ackman-2012-hlf), and one a price-elasticity model (witg-2025-vitl).

**Valuation.** 11 cases used an exit multiple on forward earnings as the main method (P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/gross profit, EV/revenue, or a sum-of-the-parts). EV, enterprise value, means equity plus net debt. 7 used a DCF (discounted cash flow) as the main or equal-weight method, and 2 had no valuation at all.

Format drives this choice. All 4 CFA winners are DCF-first. All 5 Pershing winners and all 3 Sohn winners are multiple-first. Two of the three DCF-first undergraduate winners had material arithmetic errors (cornell-ug-2024-vrt, michigan-uic-2023-de).

**Reverse builds.** Seven cases turned the price, or the consensus, into a physical quantity and then tested it:
- implied household penetration of 94% (bam-2025-frpt)
- implied load factor 2-5 points below the worst year on record (cfa-rc-2023-qan)
- required market share of about 54% (witg-2025-vitl)
- consensus implying 85% of the real market (sohn-london-2019-real)
- the bull's own model re-run with evidence-based inputs (pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr)
- plus cfa-rc-2026-lspd and cornell-ug-2024-vrt

**Rarely used tools.** Sum-of-the-parts appears once (BKR), an LBO once (LSPD), replacement cost once (LW) and Monte Carlo three times (VWS, MSI, QAN). **No case modelled backlog converting into revenue year by year**, even though three are backlog businesses (psc-2026-bkr, cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2022-msi).

## 2. How many drivers really mattered

Cases list 4-8 drivers, but in every one a single number carried the value. The sensitivity work shows how lopsided this is:
- In pro-ackman-2012-cp, 2 points of operating ratio moves value about $9-10 a share.
- In cfa-rc-2021-vws, 2 points of cost of goods erases nearly all the upside.
- In cfa-rc-2026-lspd, the team's own draft note calls margins "our big model driver".

The 2019 Dollarama winner (psc-2019-dol) put it plainly: only 1-4 things move the needle.

The "one number" is usually an operating KPI, not a price. It is often a KPI the company does not disclose, which the team rebuilt: US monthly users (DASH), pods per brewer after disclosure stopped (GMCR), effective gross margin after reclassifying labour (REAL), and remaining target employers, 465 (PGNY).

## 3. How assumptions were justified

Winners used three kinds of support, often together.

**(a) The company's own numbers, rearranged.** This is the hardest kind for a judge to dismiss, because it is the company's data.
- bam-2025-frpt: management guides revenue per household up 3-5%, but consensus needs 9-12%.
- cfa-rc-2026-lspd: guidance needs 59-71 new locations per sales rep against 35-40 historically.
- pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr: installed base rebuilt from shipment data.

**(b) An outside anchor used as a ceiling or floor.**
- Meituan's 8-9 orders a month as a ceiling (DASH)
- Utah store density and Toyota's 30% EV cap (VVV)
- US dollar-store density × market share (DOL)
- CN's operating ratio plus the Illinois Central precedent (CP)
- FY15-16 profit per pound (LW)
- the historical minimum load factor (QAN)

**(c) Primary research that closes one link.** About 90 calls (DASH), about 100 store calls (VITL), 13 expert calls (LSPD).

**Defending a gap to the Street.** The best cases printed a bridge:
- PGNY: -6% / -5% / -4% / -6% by driver
- BLBD: +7% revenue and +150bp margin
- DASH: consensus was cut 11-12% after a one-time cost and never restored

13 of 20 cases printed consensus next to their own lines. Weak spots: single-source anchors (BLBD's 5% EV-bus margin from one competitor executive; PGNY's one churned client), and consensus asserted rather than measured (VWS).

## 4. Estimate gap versus target return

For longs, the gap to consensus was small and the target was large:

| Case | Gap to consensus | Target return |
|---|---|---|
| psc-2024-vvv | EPS +2.5-3.8% | +95% |
| psc-2026-bkr | EBITDA +12%, EPS +16.5% | +63% |
| psc-2026-dash | EBITDA about +16% | +80% |
| cfa-rc-2023-qan | margin +42-60bp | +22% |
| witg-2025-blbd | revenue +7% | about +47% |

The rest of the return comes from the multiple or from duration.
- VVV and DASH said this openly and still assumed the multiple would fall (27x to 21x; 16x to 10x).
- BKR wrote "not dependent on a heroic multiple", yet its target implies P/E rising from about 17x to 23x. A judge caught it live.

Shorts carried bigger gaps: LW EBITDA -30%, PGNY gross profit -22%, GMCR long-run EPS -61%.

## 5. Scenario design and downside

**No scenario probabilities.** 13 cases gave bull, base and bear values, and 0 of 20 weighted them by probability. Monte Carlo stood in for that and gave false comfort:
- cfa-rc-2021-vws put the bear case at 6% odds, and the stock went below the bear case within 12 months.
- cfa-rc-2023-qan used a 25bp spread on load factor, when the whole debate was a 6-point swing.

**Risk/reward.** The stated ratio is a convention. Ten cases ran from 2.2x to 5.5x, with a median of about 3.4x.

**Downside cases were too tight.** Of the 13 cases with a stated bear (or loss) case, **9 saw the price breach it**. Typical causes:
- a premium multiple held fixed in the bear case (sohn-ny-2024-asml at 25x; cornell-ug-2024-vrt at 15x in every case)
- an unexplained loss cap (michigan-uic-2023-de, -8.5%)
- a bear that flexed only market share and volume (cfa-rc-2021-vws)

The downsides that held were anchored on a low multiple: DASH at 7x, DOL at 16x. psc-2019-dol also framed the downside as "what you earn if our edge is wrong", which was an 11.5% IRR in the passive case.

## 6. Valuation choices and exit multiples

Exit multiples were justified by:
- a slight premium to peers' current multiples (DASH)
- a better-fitting peer set, e.g. franchisors instead of auto retailers (VVV)
- a stated de-rating (LW)
- holding the consensus multiple fixed so that only the thesis moves the target (FRPT)

FRPT's approach is clean but self-contradictory. The thesis cuts growth, and a lower growth rate must also cut a 28x multiple. The stock fell mostly through that de-rating, while EBITDA beat the team's estimate.

The same problem shows up elsewhere:
- michigan-uic-2023-de: the target implied about 10x trough EPS, against the deck's own 17.1x history. The market paid 16-22x.
- pro-ackman-2012-cp: 14x was never justified, and the market paid about 22x.

In 9 cases the multiple moved against the call within 12 months. An exit multiple needs its own evidence, meaning the stock's history at a similar point in the cycle, not just its average.

Two related problems:
- **Stock-based pay (SBC).** psc-2026-dash counts SBC as a cash cost. A judge misread "ex-SBC" to mean the opposite, so write "after SBC". A 2021 Pershing judge showed SBC was half of one finalist's adjusted EBIT.
- **Long-dated value in short-horizon targets.** Terminal value is 83-88% of EV in cfa-rc-2021-vws and cfa-rc-2023-qan. cfa-rc-2026-lspd gave a 12-month target built on FY29-35 margins.

## 7. The model behind a 2-page memo

The memo-form winners (psc-2026-dash, psc-2026-bkr, psc-2024-vvv, psc-2019-dol) each show three things:
- **One scenario table** whose rows are the value drivers. In DOL: stores per year, same-store sales split into ticket and traffic, gross margin, P/E, then EPS, price and IRR.
- **One line of valuation arithmetic**, e.g. $12.2B × 10x → $318.
- **One or two exhibits**, such as a revenue or margin bridge.

The Fundamental Edge feedback on the WITG memos was: thesis, multiple × metric, and delta to the Street, all at the top of page one.

HFAC judges three things: thesis, "model and thesis alignment" and "logic of model assumptions". So the rows of the memo's table should be the model's input cells.

## 8. Common mistakes

**Arithmetic.** Three errors changed the answer materially:
- cornell-ug-2024-vrt added cash flows without discounting them. Corrected, the base return is +15%, not +31.7%.
- sohn-london-2019-real treated about $370M of cash as debt. Corrected, the base target is roughly flat to the price.
- michigan-uic-2023-de used $58B of debt in the WACC but $14.7B in the EV bridge.

**Hidden bridges.** 14 of 20 had at least one bridge or consistency problem. Six never showed the step from EV to per-share value (DASH, BKR, CP, VITL, LSPD, FRPT).

**Other recurring mistakes:**
- **Growth bought without its cash cost.** cfa-rc-2022-msi left acquisition spending out of free cash flow.
- **Top-down margin plugs.** MSI cut COGS 2% a year as a plug; VWS used a COGS glide path.
- **Backlog treated as volume, not margin.** VWS, BKR, MSI.
- **Management choices modelled as business drivers.** psc-2024-vvv needed about $1.8B of buybacks and had no scenario for "they buy a company instead". They did.
- **No model of how fast the effect reaches reported numbers.** sohn-ny-2019-lw needed contracts to reprice within one year when most were multi-year.

## 9. Minimal viable model spec

1. **Summary.** Price and date; target at 12 months; bull, base and bear with probabilities and an expected value; the one number next to consensus.
2. **Inputs.** Every assumption in one place, with a source column and a "why we differ from the Street" column.
3. **Build.** Segment units × price (or backlog → conversion → revenue), and segment margins.
4. **Condensed P&L and free cash flow**, after SBC, including acquisition and buyback cash.
5. **Consensus comparison** by year for the 1-2 lines the thesis changes.
6. **Valuation.** A multiple on next-year earnings, the multiple justified from its own history and peers, an explicit EV → equity → per-share bridge, and a reverse DCF.
7. **Scenarios** that flex the same drivers and the multiple, with a bear anchored on the stock's past drawdowns or trough multiple.
8. **A two-way sensitivity** of the one number against the multiple.
9. **Checks**: present values sum to the stated total, debt and share count are the same everywhere, signs are right, and the scenario base matches the summary.

What judges open first is inference. The judge behaviour recorded in the materials was to go to SBC, the cash-flow statement (psc-2022-pgny's receivables error), the most sensitive assumption, and the bridge.

## 10. Shared versus merely present

**Shared by winners:** one number, a decomposed driver, consensus printed alongside, three cases, and a roughly 3:1 stated ratio.

**Merely present:** DCF-first valuation (a CFA format habit), Monte Carlo, regressions and scraped data.

**Absent from most or all winners, and worth adding:** scenario probabilities, backlog conversion, a multiple-based downside, and explicit kill criteria (only 3 of 20: LW, FRPT, LSPD).

# How winning pitchers break the problem down: a step-by-step procedure with decision gates

## What this is based on, and how far to trust it

This synthesis draws on 20 case deep-dives in four groups:
- **MBA (Columbia's Pershing Square Challenge, PSC):** psc-2026-dash, psc-2026-bkr (2nd), psc-2024-vvv, psc-2022-pgny, psc-2019-dol.
- **Undergraduate:** witg-2025-vitl, witg-2025-blbd (3rd), bam-2025-frpt, cornell-ug-2024-vrt (2nd), michigan-uic-2023-de.
- **CFA Research Challenge global champions:** cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2022-msi, cfa-rc-2023-qan, cfa-rc-2026-lspd.
- **Professional or open-contest pitches:** sohn-ny-2019-lw, sohn-london-2019-real (a student entrant in a professional contest), sohn-ny-2024-asml, pro-ackman-2012-cp, pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr, pro-ackman-2012-hlf.

Evidence quality scores:
- One case scores 5 (sohn-ny-2019-lw).
- Fifteen score 4.
- Four score 3 (psc-2022-pgny, witg-2025-vitl, witg-2025-blbd, sohn-ny-2024-asml). No principle below rests only on these four.

Three limits apply throughout.

1. **We see winners, not losers.** Losing entries that followed the same steps are invisible.
2. **Most process is rebuilt from finished documents.** Only three teams described in their own words how they actually worked: psc-2026-dash (podcast), psc-2024-vvv (debrief) and psc-2019-dol (a team member's article). So claims about *order* and *time spent* are weaker than claims about *content*.
3. **Winning is not the same as being right.** Over roughly 12 months, about half of these calls lost money or went nowhere (Table 1).

## The procedure

Each step ends in a gate: a check you pass before moving on. For each gate I note the evidence it needs and which cases support it.

### Step 1. Screen candidates for fit with the format

Three MBA teams said they chose their stock for the competition before they had a thesis.
- **psc-2026-dash** looked at a few dozen names in 2-3 weeks. Most were vetoed in about 10 minutes. The filters were:
  - a business a judge already understands;
  - a lopsided payoff (much more to gain than to lose);
  - key debates that could be settled by research inside the course window.
- **psc-2024-vvv** dropped a healthcare name because it was "harder to research". It then kept a list of forward consensus estimates (the average Street forecast) for controversial names.
- **psc-2019-dol** listed similar rules: know the judges, and pick something you can know better than others.

Where the stock is assigned, the screen becomes "which variable inside this company is actually debated?" This applies to the CFA cases and the 4-name basket in cornell-ug-2024-vrt.

Most chosen names had a visible reason to be down or doubted:
- a guidance cut plus a named short report (psc-2019-dol);
- 15% short interest (witg-2025-blbd);
- a stock 40% off its high on an AI narrative (psc-2026-dash);
- a 32% fall after earnings (cornell-ug-2024-vrt);
- a 300x P/E beside a peer that had collapsed (psc-2022-pgny).

**Gate 1 needs:**
- a one-line business description;
- the recent price move and the stated reason for it;
- 2-3 specific research actions (calls, surveys, scrapes, filings) that could settle the main debate.

If you cannot name those actions, drop the name. Judges asked for the business description and a multi-year stock chart up front (psc-2022-pgny judge feedback).

### Step 2. Write down what the market believes, in numbers

Before building their own view, the strongest pitches pinned down the other side.
- **witg-2025-blbd** opened with "what shorts believe", sized both at 15% short interest and at a 40% gross-margin cut. The judge praised exactly this.
- **psc-2026-dash** measured a ~11-12% cut to Street EBITDA that was never reversed.
- **bam-2025-frpt** printed Street revenue year by year.
- **pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr** restated the sell-side's $9 EPS model line by line, with the source on the slide, before touching it.
- **sohn-ny-2019-lw** built a "prevailing view vs variant view" table.

Six cases then ran a reverse DCF: they worked backward from the price to the single operating number it assumes.
- **cfa-rc-2023-qan:** load factor 2.3-4.6 points below any year on record.
- **bam-2025-frpt:** 94% of management's own household market by 2034.
- **witg-2025-vitl:** ~54% share needed to hit the target.
- **sohn-london-2019-real:** 85% penetration.
- **cfa-rc-2026-lspd** and **cornell-ug-2024-vrt** also ran one.

**Gate 2 needs:** a sentence of the form "the market (source) believes X; the price implies Y; we believe Z". X and Y must be numbers.

The cases where this was missing were weak on variant view:
- cfa-rc-2021-vws asserted consensus without measuring it; its forecast simply matched guidance.
- cfa-rc-2022-msi's target was below the Street's.

Both won under a CFA rubric that rewards thoroughness. Neither found a mispricing, and neither made money over 12 months.

### Step 3. Break the headline into a product of drivers, and pick the 1-4 that carry value

Almost every case multiplied the headline out:
- users × frequency × basket (psc-2026-dash);
- capacity ÷ demand = utilization, then profit per lb (sohn-ny-2019-lw);
- employer funnel from 8,000 to 465 (psc-2022-pgny);
- installed base × cups per day × profit per cup (pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr);
- US store density × market share (psc-2019-dol);
- seats × load factor × fare (cfa-rc-2023-qan).

The discipline was to contest only the inputs you have evidence on, and to concede the rest. pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr kept the bulls' 21M-household market and fought only on usage, private label and per-cup profit. psc-2019-dol's team said there should be only "1-4 things" that move value, and made those the rows of its scenario table.

**Gate 3 needs:**
- a driver tree down to model lines;
- for each driver, your number next to consensus's number for a named year (cfa-rc-2026-lspd: FY2029 gross margin 38-39% vs 43%);
- a note on which drivers you accept as consensus.

A top-down plug fails this gate. cfa-rc-2022-msi cut COGS by 2% a year and let margins drift; the margins then missed by 4-5 points.

### Step 4. Point the research at the contested drivers only

Research was organised by driver, not by topic.
- psc-2026-dash listed the key issue in each segment and the research that could settle it, then ran ~90 calls, a 600-person survey and a scrape of ~292k listings.
- psc-2024-vvv wrote its own store-locator scraper for the white-space driver.
- witg-2025-vitl made ~100 calls to store managers for the cost (SG&A) driver.
- witg-2025-blbd's single most important data point was one competitor executive's EV-bus gross margin.

Scale varied enormously. Two placers did essentially no primary research: cornell-ug-2024-vrt (with one week to prepare) and bam-2025-frpt (desk research only). A CFA champion did none at all (cfa-rc-2021-vws). Research volume is therefore not what all winners share. What they share is that the research is tied to a driver.

Two stopping rules appear:
- **Stop when the pillar stops drawing questions.** psc-2024-vvv stopped researching unit growth once mock audiences stopped asking about it.
- **Label research that moves nothing.** cfa-rc-2026-lspd marked its 15,725-review Glassdoor analysis "No impact on valuation".

**Gate 4 needs:**
- at least two independent sources for each contested input;
- every finding either changes a model input or is labelled as context.

### Step 5. Take on the other side's best argument

The pattern is: attack the method or the hidden assumption behind the opponent's key claim, and concede what is true.
- **psc-2019-dol** showed the short seller had benchmarked a 71%-share company against a single peer.
- **witg-2025-blbd** accepted the subsidy loss but showed EV buses earn little gross profit.
- **pro-ackman-2012-cp** built a two-sided table of CP's real disadvantages. It then used management's own 200-300bp estimate to cap how much of the gap could be "structural".
- **psc-2026-dash** challenged the Citrini report's assumption about fees.

The gap in this step is the risk teams called noise:
- cfa-rc-2023-qan treated its reputation problems as media noise, then lost the 12-month trade to a regulator lawsuit and the CEO's early exit.
- psc-2026-dash left courier legal risk out of its memo. A judge raised it, and a settlement later hit the stock.

**Gate 5 needs:**
- the opponent's case in their own numbers;
- the one claim that matters most, with its method audited;
- a written list of the risks you are dismissing, with what would prove each one is not noise.

### Step 6. Value it, and split where the return comes from

Winners that later looked fragile skipped one of these checks:
- **Estimate beat vs price upside.** A judge caught psc-2026-bkr: a 12-16% beat on estimates cannot produce 63% upside without a re-rating.
- **Present value vs roll-forward.** cfa-rc-2022-msi's value today was below the price; its "upside" was one year of discount-rate accretion.
- **A multiple that fits the thesis.** bam-2025-frpt kept a 28x growth multiple while arguing growth would break. michigan-uic-2023-de needed ~10x on trough earnings against a history of 17x.
- **A bear case that flexes the axis that breaks the business.** cfa-rc-2021-vws flexed market share, not unit cost. sohn-ny-2024-asml held a 25x multiple in its bear case and saw a -39% drawdown against a -25% bear.
- **Arithmetic that ties out.** cornell-ug-2024-vrt added undiscounted cash flows. sohn-london-2019-real appears to have treated cash as debt, which moved the base target from about flat to -41%.

**Gate 6 needs:**
- a bridge from EPS change and multiple change to the return;
- a per-share bridge from EV (enterprise value) to price per share;
- a scenario table whose bear case moves the multiple;
- one reconciled set of prices and rates.

### Step 7. Decide direction and timing, then set a kill rule

None of the cases shows a formal process for choosing long vs short.
- **Shorts** tended to rest on two things. One is a temporary condition being extrapolated (witg-2025-vitl, michigan-uic-2023-de, sohn-ny-2019-lw, bam-2025-frpt). The other is disclosure hiding the economics (sohn-london-2019-real, pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr, pro-ackman-2012-hlf, cfa-rc-2026-lspd).
- **Longs** tended to rest on a temporary drag or a misclassified business.

The short cases carry a lesson about the path. Several were right on the business and still lost on the way:
- pro-ackman-2012-hlf reached +115% against the fund;
- pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr lost money overall after a -76% fall, because the fund kept adding after the collapse;
- sohn-london-2019-real had a +57% squeeze;
- sohn-ny-2019-lw went +38% against it midway.

The things that separate winning shorts from losing ones are specific:
- **Cash and forced-exit analysis.** Judges pressed psc-2022-pgny on cash and buybacks. cfa-rc-2026-lspd gave takeover risk its own slide.
- **A proof point the company itself reports, on a known date.** witg-2025-vitl tied each pillar to a print. witg-2025-blbd instead waited on a policy event, which never arrived.
- **A measurable kill rule.** Only 3 of 20 stated one: sohn-ny-2019-lw ("exit if unit margins improve as capacity ramps", which would have saved the trade), bam-2025-frpt (a flip-to-long condition) and cfa-rc-2026-lspd (buy triggers).

**Gate 7 needs:**
- a dated event inside the horizon that the company will report;
- a written condition under which you are wrong;
- for a short: cash, buybacks, takeover value and squeeze risk.

### Step 8. Treat Q&A preparation as a deliverable, and re-underwrite when the price moves

Q&A preparation was documented at scale:
- psc-2019-dol prepared 30+ questions and met no surprises;
- cfa-rc-2022-msi built ~98 appendix slides;
- cfa-rc-2026-lspd built ~155;
- cfa-rc-2023-qan built ~140;
- psc-2024-vvv ran 6-7 mock pitches.

When the price moved before the final, winners re-based openly:
- bam-2025-frpt added a "since our submission" slide after a 35% fall;
- cfa-rc-2022-msi and cfa-rc-2023-qan re-priced and re-cut their stories between rounds.

**Gate 8 needs:**
- a question bank aimed at the judge questions that recur (Table 3);
- one appendix page per likely attack.

## Shared by winners vs merely present in some

**Shared (15+ of 20):**
- a driver tree that reaches model lines;
- a stated reason the market is wrong or early;
- scenarios or sensitivities;
- a pre-emptive answer to at least one named objection.

**Common but not universal (6-13 of 20):**
- consensus stated in numbers (about 13);
- a reverse DCF (6);
- the team's own scraped or alternative data (about 8);
- field research with disclosed counts (about 8).

**Rare (3 of 20 or fewer):**
- explicit kill criteria (3);
- probability-weighted scenarios (0);
- AI or LLM tools used in the research (0). AI appears only as a thesis topic. Sentiment scoring in cfa-rc-2022-msi was decorative.

## How the four groups differ

**MBA (PSC).** They choose the stock for the format. They spend months on primary research at the largest scale in the set: psc-2026-dash made ~90 calls, psc-2019-dol ran a survey plus about ten experts. They use 3-5 year targets. They iterate against practitioner mentors. What is unique to them is the "researchable in the time window" filter and research budgets organised by segment.

**Undergraduates.** They have short preparation (about one week in cornell-ug-2024-vrt) and often a constrained list of names. Their strength is a crisp "market believes X / we find Y" frame, plus 12-month catalyst paths (witg-2025-vitl, witg-2025-blbd). Their weakness is valuation mechanics: an undefended multiple (michigan-uic-2023-de, bam-2025-frpt) and a spreadsheet error (cornell-ug-2024-vrt).

**CFA Research Challenge.** The company is assigned. Valuation is triangulated heavily (DCF plus comparables plus dividend model, and Monte Carlo in 3 of 4). Each risk comes with its valuation impact in a risk matrix (cfa-rc-2022-msi, cfa-rc-2023-qan). ESG sections are required. Appendices are huge. The weakest variant views in the set are here (cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2022-msi), because the rubric rewards completeness.

**Professionals.** They start from a framework that is reused across companies:
- the capital-cycle checklist in sohn-ny-2019-lw;
- a peer-margin outlier in pro-ackman-2012-cp;
- domain physics in sohn-ny-2024-asml.

They read disclosures forensically: dropped KPIs (pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr), costs reclassified between lines (sohn-london-2019-real), non-GAAP labels (pro-ackman-2012-hlf). They restate the bull case fairly before attacking it. Their failures are almost all about path and instrument (size, timing, a third-party catalyst), not analysis.

## Caveat on outcomes

Judges reward process, evidence and Q&A (see the competition notes). Over the pitch's horizon:
- **Worked cleanly:** witg-2025-vitl, bam-2025-frpt and pro-ackman-2012-cp.
- **Lost badly:** psc-2024-vvv, cfa-rc-2021-vws and sohn-ny-2024-asml, all of which were winners.

The most common failure was being right on the business while the multiple or the path went the other way:
- psc-2026-bkr, witg-2025-blbd, michigan-uic-2023-de and cfa-rc-2023-qan did this within 12 months;
- sohn-ny-2024-asml was right only after about 2 years.

Treat the procedure as the way to build a defensible pitch, not as proof of an edge.

# Lens: AI, ML and alternative data: who used it, what exactly, whether it drove, supported or decorated the thesis, how it was 

# AI, ML and alternative data in winning stock pitches

## Bottom line

- **No winning pitch in this corpus used AI, LLMs, AI agents or machine learning to do its research.** At least, none says so. That holds for all 20 deep-dive cases and for the discovery findings.
- **"AI" shows up as a subject of the thesis, not as a tool.** Examples: the AI-disruption rebuttal (psc-2026-dash), AI demand for chips (sohn-ny-2024-asml, cornell-ug-2024-vrt), and AI scenarios (cfa-rc-2026-lspd).
- **Winners' computational work is plain.** They write a scraper, join it to a government denominator, and count. Or they code complaints by type. Or they buy a transaction dataset and compare it with a control.
- **The data mattered most when it set one number the model depends on.** It mattered least when it was a big count on a credibility slide.
- **Data work did not predict getting paid.** Several of the most data-driven pitches lost money over the holding period.

"We found almost nothing" is the honest answer on AI tools. The finding is still useful: in this corpus, AI-assisted research is not what wins.

## What we looked at

We checked 20 deep-dive cases: 4 Pershing Square, 4 CFA Research Challenge, 5 undergraduate, 3 Sohn and 3 professional. We also grepped the saved raw text for ChatGPT, LLM, machine learning, generative AI, scrape and Python. That search added three discovery items:
- a 2025 WITG runner-up short on Udemy (witg_fe2025_2nd_udmy);
- a 2016 CFA champion report (cfa_rc_2016_waterloo);
- the one AI disclosure we found, in the 2025 CFA champion report (cfa_rc_2025_kozminski).

Discovery items were not deep-dived. They carry less weight.

## Finding 1: no disclosed use of AI or ML as a research tool

- **Zero of 20 deep-dive cases describe using an LLM, AI agent or ML model in their research.**
- **The one direct test is psc-2024-vvv.** A finals judge asked the Valvoline team outright, "did you use AI?". They answered with their method instead: R code scraping store locators, a Google Maps API, and Census radius joins. That was 2024, before the current rules.
- **Only one AI disclosure appears anywhere we read.** The 2025 CFA champion (cfa_rc_2025_kozminski) says LLMs were used "to proofread … only". All analysis and conclusions were the team's own.
- **The 2026 CFA champion made no disclosure at all.** Both cfa-rc-2026-lspd documents were grepped for AI, LLM and ChatGPT, and neither contains a disclosure. This is despite CFA's 2025-26 rules requiring disclosure of material AI reliance.
- **The only AI-first contest found is the 2026 Perplexity Computer contest.** It required AI. Its winner and judging criteria were not found, and a Wall Street Oasis thread on the finals was negative about quality. It is not evidence either way for a human-judged, AI-restricted contest.

**Caveat:** absence of disclosure is not proof of non-use. Most documents predate the rules that force disclosure. What we can say is that no winner presented AI use as part of its edge.

## Finding 2: what winners actually used

Non-AI alternative data is common. The main types:

- **Self-built scrapes joined to public denominators:**
  - psc-2024-vvv: store locators joined to state vehicle registrations and Census population.
  - psc-2026-dash: ~292k DoorDash and Uber Eats merchant listings.
  - witg-2025-blbd: a ~390k-bus registration dataset.
  - cfa_rc_2016_waterloo: store locations, postal-code census data and Google Maps drive times, used to test the company's "90% of Canadians within 15 minutes" claim.
  - witg_fe2025_2nd_udmy: 3,400 courses scraped and bucketed into high, medium or low AI risk by keyword search.
- **Scrapes of reviews and complaints, coded by type:** cfa-rc-2026-lspd, with 5,852 BBB complaints coded by type and 15,725 Glassdoor reviews.
- **Commissioned transaction data with a control:** pro-ackman-2012-hlf. About 39k usable eBay canister sales were compared against a GNC control product.
- **Labour-market triangulation:** sohn-london-2019-real. Filing headcount × LinkedIn role mix × Glassdoor salary gave a restated gross margin.
- **Hand-built trackers and screens:**
  - sohn-ny-2019-lw: a plant-by-plant capacity tracker.
  - psc-2022-pgny: an employer-by-employer screen of about 8,000 employers.
- **Panels and government series:**
  - bam-2025-frpt: Nielsen, AVMA survey data and WhaleWisdom.
  - cfa-rc-2023-qan: Google Destination Insights.
  - michigan-uic-2023-de: used-equipment prices and dealer inventory days.
- **Surveys:**
  - psc-2026-dash: n=600.
  - psc-2019-dol: n=120.
  - cfa-rc-2026-lspd: 52 merchants and about 135 consumers.
  - cfa-rc-2022-msi: respondent count never disclosed.

The "quant" methods are classical: Monte Carlo (cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2022-msi, cfa-rc-2023-qan), OLS regressions (cfa-rc-2022-msi, psc-2019-dol), a Poisson outbreak model (witg-2025-vitl), and a dictionary-based sentiment count (cfa-rc-2022-msi). None is ML in the modern sense.

## Finding 3: drive, support or decorate

**DROVE a thesis pillar (5 cases).** In each, the data set the one number the pillar depends on:
- pro-ackman-2012-hlf: the 35% retail discount, the largest adjustment.
- sohn-london-2019-real: the 48.7% effective gross margin.
- psc-2024-vvv: the 9,200-store lower bound on white space.
- sohn-ny-2019-lw: the utilization path.
- psc-2022-pgny: the 465 employers left to win.

**SUPPORTED one link in a chain (about 11 cases).** Examples:
- psc-2026-dash: the scrape and survey backed "best product → share gains → MAU growth". The driver tree and ~90 calls carried the thesis.
- witg-2025-blbd: the registration data backed captive demand. One competitor-executive call drove the thesis.
- bam-2025-frpt: backed the TAM and catalyst timing.
- cfa-rc-2023-qan: Google data corroborated a reverse DCF.
- Also psc-2019-dol, michigan-uic-2023-de, pro-ackman-2012-cp and pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr.

**DECORATED (4 cases).**
- cfa-rc-2022-msi: the sentiment count sits in one appendix slide, feeds nothing, and has no benchmark. The Monte Carlo produced 38% "sell" outcomes and was still used as support.
- cfa-rc-2021-vws: the Monte Carlo put the bear case at 6%. The stock fell 33% in 12 months, below the bear case.
- cfa-rc-2023-qan: the Monte Carlo used a 25bp load-factor standard deviation while the debate was a 6-point swing.
- cfa-rc-2026-lspd: the team itself labelled its Glassdoor work "No impact on valuation".

**NONE:** psc-2026-bkr, cornell-ug-2024-vrt and sohn-ny-2024-asml.

The pattern: data that drives is narrow and aimed at one assumption. Big counts are usually support or decoration. LSPD's 21,611 datapoints did not produce its key driver, which came from sales-rep capacity arithmetic on company disclosures.

## Finding 4: how it was presented, and how judges reacted

**Presentation.** The strongest pattern is one number on the main slide and the method in the appendix.
- psc-2024-vvv put one line on the white-space slide ("Based on Locations Data Scraping Exercise"). Six appendix slides held the method, and the team pulled them up when asked.
- psc-2026-dash used one slide that "slice[d] this several different ways" (survey, scrape, third-party data).
- pro-ackman-2012-hlf footnoted its filtering rules (68,286 raw transactions down to 39,396 usable) and named a control.
- sohn-london-2019-real used one four-column table: headcount × salary = cost = % of sales.

The opposite style is cfa-rc-2026-lspd. It opened with a credibility slide of counts ("21,611 datapoints scraped", "188 customer interviews"), but 136 of those "interviews" were survey responses and the scraping method is never described.

**Judge reaction.** Evidence here is thin: three data points.
1. psc-2024-vvv: a judge asked for the method and whether AI was used. A second judge probed whether low-density areas just mean more competition.
2. A Balyasny 2025 co-winner (Indiana, discovery, press only): a judge asked to see the team's code.
3. witg_fe2025_2nd_udmy (discovery): the Fundamental Edge reviewer singled out the scrape as the key proof.

In psc-2026-dash, no recorded judge question touched the scrape or survey. The podcast host praised the ~90 calls and the Wolt case study instead. For every other case, judge reaction to the data is not disclosed.

What judges *do* visibly ask is "how does this reach the numbers, and when?" (witg-2025-vitl, first question) and "why is it mispriced?" (discovery commentary). Data that cannot answer those questions is unlikely to be credited.

## Finding 5: the rules

- **HFAC x Citadel (rule 10).** AI may be used for initial research only if clearly disclosed. The submission must not be generated "in whole or in part" by generative AI; ChatGPT, OpenAI models, Copilot and Vertex AI are named. Grammar check is allowed; Copilot in Word is not.
- **CFA RC 2025-26.** Allows AI with documentation. Teams must acknowledge, verify, evaluate and reflect, and judges are told to ask about it.
- **Wharton Global Youth.** AI is allowed for brainstorming, and AI material must be cited.
- **Sohn (2021-22 rules), McGill, Miami, Michigan UIC, BAM.** Say nothing on AI.
- **Perplexity.** Requires AI.

No past HFAC/Citadel winning memo is public, so we cannot see how a winner handled the disclosure there. All we know is the rule.

## Finding 6: credible uses and gimmicks

**Credible uses share four traits:**
1. **A named claim to test.** The data tests a specific claim from management or consensus: the store-reach claim (cfa_rc_2016_waterloo), the $200bn TAM (sohn-london-2019-real), "retail profit" (pro-ackman-2012-hlf), 42M households (bam-2025-frpt), 8,000 employers (psc-2022-pgny).
2. **A public denominator.** Registrations, Census counts or filings (psc-2024-vvv, witg-2025-blbd).
3. **A control or a stated bias.** The GNC control in pro-ackman-2012-hlf. The Udemy team admitting its keyword bank was not exhaustive (witg_fe2025_2nd_udmy).
4. **An output that is one model input.**

**Gimmicks share the opposite traits:**
- The output feeds no line: the MSI sentiment count, the LSPD Glassdoor work.
- Undisclosed samples: the MSI survey, the DASH scraper method.
- Simulations that restate their inputs: vws, msi, qan.
- Formal probabilities on rare, clustered events: the witg-2025-vitl Poisson model, where the team admitted in Q&A that the risk cannot really be quantified.

**Figures can conflict inside one pitch.** In witg_fe2025_2nd_udmy, the spoken pitch says 58% of top courses are at high AI risk, while the reviewer reads "a little over 3/4" off the summary. Check that every figure from a tool-built dataset appears the same way everywhere.

## Finding 7: data depth did not predict who got paid

Survivorship warning. Outcomes for the five cases where data DROVE the thesis:

| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| psc-2024-vvv | -31% vs the S&P +51% |
| pro-ackman-2012-hlf | Stock +115% against the short at exit |
| sohn-london-2019-real | +57% against the short before the thesis paid off |
| sohn-ny-2019-lw | +38% against the short within eight months |
| psc-2022-pgny | Target reached 27 months late |

In each case the data proved something about the business's state: the white space existed, recruiting dominated, costs were hidden. It did not prove the timing. In VVV the data answered a question that did not bind within the horizon.

The data that did line up with the payoff was **high-frequency data that runs ahead of a reported line**:
- bam-2025-frpt: a Nielsen release was a named catalyst, and the stock fell about 12% on one.
- witg-2025-vitl: store-manager calls ahead of guidance cuts.
- michigan-uic-2023-de: used prices ahead of earnings. EPS fell, but the stock still rose.

## How to present tool-gathered data so it reads as rigour

These points come from the credible cases above. The rule compliance points restate the HFAC rule itself.

1. **Say which claim the data tests** (management's figure, or the consensus assumption), in one sentence.
2. **Report the result as one number next to consensus,** in the model line it changes (hlf, real, vvv).
3. **Give sample, dates, filters and a control or benchmark in one footnote.** Keep a method appendix, or a model tab, you can open in Q&A (vvv, hlf).
4. **State the known bias and which direction it cuts** (witg_fe2025_2nd_udmy).
5. **Label anything that does not move value as context,** as cfa-rc-2026-lspd did with Glassdoor. Or cut it.
6. **Under HFAC rules:** disclose any AI used in initial research in plain words, and make sure no memo or model text is AI-generated. The code and the data joins you wrote yourself are your evidence of human work. Expect a judge to ask about them, as in psc-2024-vvv.

## Where evidence is thin

- Judge reactions to data are recorded in only three places.
- No team described using an LLM, so we cannot say how judges would react to disclosed AI use.
- Scraper methods are undisclosed in psc-2026-dash and cfa-rc-2026-lspd.
- The Perplexity contest winner and results are unknown.

# Lens: What judges reward, and whether winners were right: published criteria, what separates winners from finalists, memo stru

# What judges reward, and whether winners were right

## Bottom line

- **Judges score the process, not the result.** Every published rubric rewards reasoning, evidence, and how well the model matches the thesis. None can reward accuracy, because the result arrives months after the prize.
- **Winning calls are close to a coin flip over 3-12 months.** 15 cases have a price reading at about 12 months. 8 moved the way the pitch said and 7 did not (Table 2).
- **Almost none were right for the reason they gave.** Only witg-2025-vitl was clearly right on price *through its stated mechanism* inside 12 months. bam-2025-frpt and cfa-rc-2026-lspd were partly right that way. The other price "wins" came from COVID (sohn-ny-2019-lw, sohn-london-2019-real), from the opposite mechanism (cornell-ug-2024-vrt), or from a revenue miss while the core thesis later failed (pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr).
- **The most common way a winner lost money:** it was right about the business and wrong about the **multiple** or the **timing**. The multiple is the price investors pay per dollar of earnings. Timing means the catalyst arrived late. This is what happened in michigan-uic-2023-de, sohn-ny-2024-asml, witg-2025-blbd, cfa-rc-2023-qan and psc-2026-bkr, and in the Carlisle 2025 case already studied.

## 1. The published criteria

Table 1 sets the rubrics side by side. Three points matter most.

- **HFAC x Citadel names only three criteria:** quality and viability of the thesis, model-thesis alignment, and logic of the model assumptions. Nothing rewards polish in round 1 (Citadel rules PDF, discovery).
- **Where weights are published, thesis and valuation dominate.** McGill puts 60% on thesis plus valuation and 15% on Q&A. The CFA Research Challenge puts 40% of the report on valuation plus financial analysis, and 20% of the presentation on Q&A.
- **The Sohn contest is the closest in shape to HFAC.** It is blinded, at most 4 pages, with a 12-month horizon, and it asks for "a carefully reasoned analysis" that fits current market conditions.

**Past HFAC winners.** No past HFAC/Citadel winning memo is public. Four discovery lanes searched the HFAC site, Citadel, Instagram, LinkedIn and the open web and found none. The psc-2026-dash search also found none. This supports the claim but does not prove it.

**AI rules.** Citadel allows AI for initial research only if you disclose it, and bans generated text. The CFA challenge allows AI with documented disclosure. None of the 20 cases shows AI used in its research. In 2024 a Pershing judge asked the psc-2024-vvv team "Did you use AI?" about its scraper, so expect the question.

## 2. What judges say separates winners from finalists

- **Q&A under pressure and a conservative exit multiple.** In Pershing 2020, EPAM beat Sysco on tough questions, a lower exit multiple, and a credible growth case. A finalist lost ground for ignoring disruption risk. Another was faulted for valuing only against comparables. (Discovery; not a deep-dive case, so this is a weaker source.)
- **Depth of primary research.** Pershing 2010 and 2012 judges cited it. The psc-2026-dash team made ~90 calls, and its podcast host singled out the Wolt case study.
- **Show the other side of the trade, then your delta to the Street.** Fundamental Edge said the witg-2025-blbd summary worked because it opened with "here's what the market thinks". The witg-2025-vitl summary tied the thesis "directly to what breaks".
- **Translate the story into numbers and dates.** The first finals question in witg-2025-vitl was when the thesis would hit the market, and what in guidance or consensus had to change.
- **Is the upside consistent with the beat?** A psc-2026-bkr judge asked why a ~16% estimate beat should produce +63%.

## 3. How winners structure the memo

The Pershing 2-page memos (psc-2026-dash, psc-2026-bkr, psc-2024-vvv, psc-2019-dol) share one order:

1. The recommendation with a target and an IRR (the annual return it implies).
2. A one-paragraph business description.
3. 3-4 numbered theses.
4. Valuation with bear, base and bull cases.
5. Risks and mitigants.

The first lines state the one number. psc-2026-dash opens with a 14% GOV CAGR built as users x frequency x basket (GOV is gross order value, total spend on the platform). psc-2019-dol opens with "8x more residents, 17x more dollar stores".

Other patterns:
- **Put the thesis, the multiple x metric, and the delta vs Street in one place at the top.** This was Fundamental Edge's critique of witg-2025-blbd.
- **Put a stock chart up front.** Pershing 2022 judges told every team this (psc-2022-pgny).
- **Keep the heavy material in an appendix for Q&A.** psc-2024-vvv held its scraper method in the appendix, cfa-rc-2026-lspd had ~155 backup slides, and psc-2019-dol prepared 30+ answers.
- **Do not copy the CFA report format.** CFA reports run ~21 pages with ESG sections. That format does not transfer to a 2-page memo (cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2023-qan).

## 4. Q&A: what judges ask, and which answers worked

Questions cluster into a few themes (Table 4):
- Legal and labour exposure (psc-2026-dash courier classification).
- Beat size vs upside size (psc-2026-bkr).
- SBC treatment. SBC is stock-based pay, a real cost that "adjusted" profit often leaves out (psc-2026-dash).
- Cash and buybacks on a short (psc-2022-pgny).
- Management discretion (psc-2024-vvv franchise and refranchising).
- Data method (psc-2024-vvv).
- Input costs and holder overhang (witg-2025-blbd).

**Answers that worked** pulled prepared evidence. psc-2024-vvv opened its appendix scraping charts. psc-2019-dol said it faced "no surprise question".

**Answers that hurt:**
- A reframing non-answer on why management cut store openings (psc-2019-dol).
- "No way to really quantify" the flu risk (witg-2025-vitl).
- "We'll get back to them" on sponsor selling (witg-2025-blbd).
- A conceded receivables error in the cash-flow model (psc-2022-pgny).
- One wording slip: the judge heard "ex-SBC" as the opposite of what the team meant (psc-2026-dash).

**Risks judges raise often show up later.** Courier pay hit DoorDash in September 2026. Refranchising cut Valvoline's reported revenue.

## 5. Outcome track record

Table 2 has prices at pitch, about 6 months, about 12 months, and about 24 months where we could verify them.

- **About 12 months: 8 right on price, 7 wrong or flat.**
  - Right: psc-2022-pgny, cfa-rc-2026-lspd (9 months), witg-2025-vitl, bam-2025-frpt, cornell-ug-2024-vrt, sohn-ny-2019-lw, sohn-london-2019-real, pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr.
  - Wrong or flat: cfa-rc-2021-vws, cfa-rc-2022-msi (down in absolute terms but +14 points vs SPY), cfa-rc-2023-qan, witg-2025-blbd, michigan-uic-2023-de, sohn-ny-2024-asml, pro-ackman-2012-hlf.
- **Right on price but for another reason:** psc-2022-pgny (the business beat for two years), sohn-ny-2019-lw and sohn-london-2019-real (COVID), cornell-ug-2024-vrt (the stock traded *more* as an AI proxy, the opposite of the pitch's wedge).
- **Right about the business, wrong on the stock:**
  - michigan-uic-2023-de: FY24 EPS came in at $25.62, below even the team's $28.56, yet the stock rose 7% in 12 months and 80% by now.
  - witg-2025-blbd: margins held, but the stock was about flat while the S&P rose 15%.
  - sohn-ny-2024-asml: memory orders arrived 18-24 months late, after a -36% year.
  - cfa-rc-2023-qan: the business beat by far, but an ACCC lawsuit flattened the stock.
- **Right eventually, too late for the horizon:** sohn-ny-2024-asml, sohn-ny-2019-lw (the supply bust came in 2023-25), sohn-london-2019-real (after a +57% squeeze), psc-2022-pgny, pro-ackman-2012-hlf (fell in 2024, years after the exit).

**What this says.** Judges reward process. Process is necessary, but it does not predict 12-month accuracy. The discovery lanes agree: CFA champions' 12-month outcomes were mixed (Vestas -32%, VAT -22%, Copa +47%), and several placed undergraduate pitches lost (GOOS, LYV, SG).

## 6. Failure modes

**Winners that were wrong:**

1. **The multiple was left undefended or held fixed.**
   - michigan-uic-2023-de's target implied about 10x trough EPS. The market paid 16-22x.
   - bam-2025-frpt held 28x while arguing that growth would break. The stock fell more than its model allowed.
   - The sohn-ny-2024-asml bear case kept 25x, but the realised fall came from de-rating: -39%, not the -25% bear case.
2. **The catalyst depended on a third party.**
   - witg-2025-blbd needed the EPA to cut funding. The money kept flowing.
   - pro-ackman-2012-hlf needed the FTC to shut the company down. It settled instead.
   - sohn-ny-2024-asml's Investor Day confirmed the old 2030 range.
3. **Backlog was treated as protection, not as a flow with costs.** Backlog means signed orders not yet delivered.
   - cfa-rc-2021-vws: a fixed-price backlog met cost inflation.
   - psc-2026-bkr: record orders, flat revenue.
   - cfa-rc-2022-msi: backlog was not modelled.
4. **Management discretion went unstressed.**
   - psc-2024-vvv: buybacks were swapped for a debt-funded acquisition.
   - bam-2025-frpt: management cut capex to protect margins.
   - psc-2026-bkr: management's Chart acquisition numbers were taken as given.
5. **A risk dismissed as "noise" became the kill risk.** cfa-rc-2023-qan called the reputation damage "the Joe Aston effect", and the ACCC then sued.
6. **Arithmetic errors survived judging.**
   - cornell-ug-2024-vrt added undiscounted cash flows. Corrected, the base value falls from +32% to about +15%.
   - sohn-london-2019-real appears to have treated cash as debt, which moves the base target from $10.8 to about $18.
   - cfa-rc-2022-msi booked acquisition-driven revenue without subtracting the acquisition cash.

**Famous professional failures:**
- **pro-ackman-2012-hlf:** no horizon, no scenario where the company survives, and a position about 20% of shares outstanding. That invited a counter-buyer.
- **pro-einhorn-2011-gmcr:** the stock fell 76% early, but the patent-moat thesis failed through channel power (Keurig 2.0). Greenlight added to the short at lower prices and lost money.
- **pro-ackman-2012-cp succeeded, but not only on its own logic.** The operating ratio target was hit a year early. (The operating ratio is operating cost as a share of revenue, so lower is better.) But the peer CN also doubled, and the multiple rose from 14x to about 22x.

## 7. What winners share vs what merely appears

**Shared by nearly all 20:**
- A recommendation and target stated early (19 of 20; the pro-ackman-2012-hlf deck has no written target).
- Named risks.
- A quantified wedge.
- Consensus stated in numbers (14 of 20, plus 3 partial).
- Explicit scenario values (13 of 20).

**Merely appears:**
- Alternative data (about 8 of 20).
- Monte Carlo simulation (3, all CFA; each gave false precision).
- DCF as the primary valuation method (7).
- Stated kill criteria (3: sohn-ny-2019-lw, bam-2025-frpt, cfa-rc-2026-lspd).
- Scenario probabilities (0).
- AI in research (0).

## 8. Survivorship caveats

- **The sample is biased toward cases with public materials,** so it favours prestigious competitions and famous professionals.
- **Judges' deliberations are recorded in only a few cases.** We infer "why it won" from what winners did, not from judges' scores.
- **Losing finalists are almost never published.** We cannot tell whether finalists shared the same traits.
- **Several outcomes are too early to judge** (psc-2026-dash, psc-2026-bkr), or have missing 6- and 24-month points.
- **The professional cases were chosen because they are famous.** That favours dramatic outcomes in both directions.
