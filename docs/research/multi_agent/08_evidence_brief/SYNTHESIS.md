## Evidence brief: GE Vernova (GEV), for the team's own memo and model

**Why there is no memo text in this brief.** The HFAC x Citadel rules allow AI for initial research if it is disclosed. They ban generated text in any part of the submission (PLAYBOOK.md s.7; GEV_GAP_CHECK row "HFAC rule 10"). So this brief gives facts, tests, arithmetic and ranges. It does not give thesis lines, and it does not choose forecasts. Wherever a forecast is needed, the direction lookup has a row for each choice, and the team picks the row. Every number has a fact ID. The fact table gives its source and locator so that team members can re-derive it by hand before it goes into the memo or model.

**Basis used throughout.** Price is $962.49, the close on 2026-09-29. The balance sheet is at 2026-06-30 (Q2'26 10-Q, 0001996810-26-000148). "Consensus" means MarketScreener unless a line says otherwise. Adjusted EBITDA is GEV's non-GAAP measure, and MarketScreener's EBITDA line matches it (CT-03).

### 1. What was tested and why

Eleven research lanes each ran a test, and a second pass re-derived each lane's numbers. The tests answer five questions.

1. Is GLJ's bear case right about 2027 margins? GLJ is the one public Sell, with a $470 target.
2. Is the team's backlog-vintage rebuttal of GLJ correct, and is it new?
3. What does $962.49 already assume?
4. Where could the team's number differ from consensus, and on what date would that show?
5. What sets the size of the bear case?

The earlier gap check found that the work was "a well-evidenced rebuttal, not yet a pitch with an edge". The tests below were chosen to find out whether that is still true.

### 2. The decisive tests and their results

**Test 1. Is the Power margin climb a mix effect?** (PMX-07, PMX-33, PMX-34)

Power segment EBITDA margin went from 9.9% (FY23) to 17.6% (H1'26). A mix effect means the margin rose because the higher-margin services line grew as a share of revenue. It did not. The services share *fell*, from 67.9% to 63.1%. With services at any assumed margin between 20% and 35%, the mix effect is negative, from -0.5 to -2.8 points. The whole climb therefore happened inside the categories.

This rules out "mix" as the rival explanation. It does not show whether the gain came from equipment or from services pricing. The CFO says services contracts are also being repriced (PMX-31).

About 2 of the 4.8-point FY23-FY25 climb is currency and disposals, not organic (PMX-17, PMX-18). The H1'26 gain is fully organic (PMX-19).

**Test 2. What does GLJ's "about 3 points" on old equipment require?** (PMX-32, PMX-44, Grid F)

GEV does not disclose equipment and services margins inside Power. The implied H1'26 equipment EBITDA margin falls to 3% if services earn 26.2%. It is 5.0% at 25% and 13.6% at 20%. Siemens Energy's gas services business has the same 66% services share. Its numbers imply services margins of roughly 19-25% for new-unit margins between +5% and -5%. On that peer anchor, implied GEV Power equipment margin is about 5-14%. This test cannot settle the question. It rests on a number GEV does not publish.

**Test 3. What are GLJ's 3 and 10-11 points?** (GLJ-02, GLJ-08, GLJ-09, PMX-29 corrected, VF-05)

GLJ's figures match exactly the yearly *changes* in GEV's average Power backlog margin: +3 in FY24 and +11 in FY25, from the Q4'25 deck, slide 5. GEV defines that measure as the change in the average estimated **contribution margin** of the whole backlog. Contribution margin is price minus variable cost, before fixed costs. It is not EBITDA margin. GLJ's note was not seen, so the match is an inference.

Using GEV's own increments, and removing the EDF backlog that was sold, gives these implied margins by order year, in contribution points above a roughly breakeven YE2022 base:
- 2023 orders: about 5-6 points.
- 2024 orders: about 7.5 points (about 5.5 if the oldest backlog ships first).
- 2025 orders: about 19-20 points.

Two conclusions follow. GLJ's "3" for the 2024 book is below what GEV's own disclosures imply. But the gap between old and new orders, about 12-15 points, is *larger* than GLJ's 7.5. On the size of the vintage effect, GLJ's direction holds.

**Test 4. When does the 2025 book ship? This is the timing dispute.** (GLJ-21, GLJ-12, VF-02, GLJ-16 corrected)

The RPO note says 32% of the YE2025 equipment backlog, $20,558M, is expected as revenue in 2027. RPO (remaining performance obligations) is contracted revenue not yet recognized. That figure holds on its face.

Three findings weaken its use against GLJ.
- On 2026-09-16, management mapped backlog-margin changes to P&L margins three to four years later. The CEO said the 2026 change "tells you the equipment margins for '29 and '30, largely" (VF-02). On the Q4'25 call, management said the majority of the higher-margin orders from 2024 and 2025 deliver in "2027 and beyond" (GLJ-12). Both statements are closer to GLJ's lag than to the team's cut.
- RPO shares weight each vintage by dollars. The margin gain sits in heavy-duty gas, which has the longest lead times. So a vintage's share of 2027 revenue is not its share of 2027 margin.
- The team's "at most $5.8B of pre-2025 Power book left" is a floor, not a cap. The true remainder at YE2025 lies between $5,775M and $12,461M.

The one-year conversion rate has also missed by about 10% in each direction: 91.8% of prediction in 2024 and 110.5% in 2025 (POS-21).

**Test 5. How much of GLJ's EBITDA gap can the vintage argument close?** (GLJ-27, GLJ-26 corrected)

GLJ's FY27 EBITDA is $7,420M. GLJ puts the Street at $9,450M; MarketScreener shows $9,574M. GLJ's stated Power margin gap (18.5% against a GLJ-quoted 20.7% consensus) is worth only $0.55-0.62B, which is 27-30% of GLJ's $2.03B gap. If the vintage argument won completely, it would bring GLJ to about $7.97-8.04B. The other ~$1.4-1.5B has no public explanation. The team cannot claim to refute GLJ's number in full.

**Test 6. Is GLJ in the price?** (BX-09, POS-05, VF-11, POS-02)

The stock fell 8.6% on 2026-09-14. Vertiv, Eaton and Siemens Energy fell 7.6-8.0% the same day. That day also had an AI-slowdown call from the model provider's CEO and a 10-year Treasury yield at 5%. Against a Vertiv/Eaton basket, GEV's excess move was -3.3% (t = -1.36, not significant). Against the S&P 500 alone it was -7.8% (t = -2.75, significant). So the answer depends on the benchmark.

By 09-29 the stock had recovered 106% of the drop and closed $5.22 above the 09-11 close. Whatever GLJ discount existed is no longer visible in the price.

**Test 7. Is the vintage idea new to the market?** (POS-12, POS-14, POS-20)

No. GEV publishes "change in margin in backlog" every January. Wolfe asked about it on the Q4'25 call. GLJ built its case on it, and the RPO schedule is in every 10-Q. No public source was found that splits 2027 revenue by vintage using the RPO dollars. Paywalled notes were not checked.

**Test 8. What does $962.49 require?** (RV-12, RV-22, VF-07, VF-08)

EV (enterprise value: equity plus debt minus cash, plus minority interests) is $247.2B on the simple bridge and $250.7B on the fuller one. That is 25.8x FY27 consensus EBITDA and 38.7x FY27 EPS, about 2.0x the S&P 500's 19.2x.

Reverse test (VF-07 is computed from verified inputs):
- Management's 2028 framework ($11.2B EBITDA) supports today's price only at a year-end-2028 exit multiple of 25.4-28.3x.
- Consensus FY28 EBITDA ($13,064M) needs 21.8-24.3x.
- The consensus target of $1,237.34 equals about 26.3x next-twelve-month EBITDA at Sept 2027, or 38.8x next-twelve-month EPS. Today those are 28.2x and 42.6-46.9x.

So the consensus target does not need multiple expansion. It needs consensus earnings delivered while the forward multiple holds or eases slightly. The downside case is compression: at 20x next-twelve-month EBITDA on consensus, the Sept-2027 value is about $950 (table C).

**Test 9. Consensus against management.** (CT-29, BR-30, BR-28)

Street FY28 revenue is 8.0% above management's $56B framework. Street EBITDA is 16.6% above $11.2B, and the Street margin is 21.6% against the framework's 20%. Management has raised the framework twice, both times at December events: +4 margin points (Dec-2024) and +6 points plus 15.6% revenue (Dec-2025). The Jan-2026 step from $52B to $56B was the Prolec acquisition, not organic growth.

Consensus therefore already assumes roughly one more framework raise: about half the Dec-2025 revenue raise and about a quarter of its margin raise. The first-year revenue beats against the first guide were only +1.3% (FY24) and +4.3% (FY25). No December 2026 event is listed (RQ-44). The CEO says the FY27 outlook comes on the January 2027 call, with a Capital Markets Day and 2030 outlook in spring 2027 (PP-03, PP-07).

**Test 10. The H2'26 and Q3 hurdle.** (RQ-08 corrected, PP-18, PP-19, VF-10, RQ-10)

The FY26 guide implies an H2 adjusted EBITDA margin of 13.2-16.9%. H1 was 10.5%. Consensus FY26 implies 16.3% for H2, which is above the guide midpoint of 15.0%.

For Q3, consensus is $12,013M revenue and $1,706M EBITDA. That sits at the top of a guide-implied range the lane built itself, $1,535-1,716M, which depends on its own assumptions for the Electrification margin and the Corporate line. MarketScreener's Q4'26 row implies an 18.2% margin.

Wind has to earn about +$257M in H2 to meet its ~-$400M FY guide. It lost $657M in H1 and $286M in H2'25. The last positive Wind quarter was Q4'24, at +$19M.

Q3 reports on 2026-10-28, which is **after** the finals on 10-23.

**Test 11. Cash quality.** (CQ-13, CQ-15, CQ-43, VF-12)

Operating cash flow excluding the rise in contract liabilities was negative in every period: FY23 -$1.6B (a pre-spin carve-out year), FY24 -$0.2B, FY25 -$3.0B, H1'26 -$3.0B. Contract liabilities are mainly customer down payments. Excluding *all* working capital, the figures are +$0.06B, +$1.5B, +$0.9B and -$1.0B. So the deposits-only cut is the harshest view.

FCF divided by adjusted EBITDA goes from 1.16x (FY25) to about 2.0x at the FY26 guide midpoint. Consensus FY27 FCF of $7,417M is already 38% below FY26 (single source).

SBC is small: $257M in FY25 and $135M in H1'26. It is mostly not added back to adjusted EBITDA. Separation costs, which are added back, include a one-time stock option grant of undisclosed size.

**Test 12. Customer pricing evidence.** (TP-05, VF-03, VF-04, TP-21, TP-12)

In the one same-utility, same-site, same-size GE pair (LG&E/KU), all-in plant cost rose 53-57%. That compares a GE order announced 2024-04-11 with a power-island contract signed 2026-06-23. It includes three to four years of cost escalation to later in-service dates.

The Brown 12 $25M reservation was paid in part "to lock in firm pricing for the equipment." That cuts against the reading that GE reset prices when the reservation converted to an order.

Customers with framework agreements show little change from 2024 to 2025: Entergy Mississippi 0%, Duke about +5%. Neither filing names its OEM. LG&E/KU cites 2026 market indications of $2,800-3,200/kW for projects without reservations. All of this is plant-level cost. None of it isolates GE's turbine price.

**Test 13. Reservations against firm backlog.** (RQ-14, RQ-15, RQ-16)

Of GEV's 116 GW, 63 GW (54%) is slot reservation agreements (SRAs). These are paid reservations, not firm orders, and they are not in RPO. At Siemens Energy the share is 27%. GEV converts 14-28% of opening SRAs into orders each quarter, and 34 GW converted over five quarters. GEV's firm gas backlog of 53 GW is below Siemens Energy's 69 GW.

**Test 14. Sizing the bear case from the stock's own history.** (BX-03 to BX-08, VF-01, BX-17)

Since the spin there have been five closing drawdowns of 15% or more, meaning falls from a running high to a low: -38.3%, -17.5%, -15.0%, -24.6% and -25.5%. The median is -24.6%. Three of them happened while the S&P 500 rose.

Chartloom next-twelve-month P/E troughs run from 33.8x to 51.1x. Chartloom next-twelve-month EV/EBITDA troughs run from 15.6x to 28.5x. Both series apply later estimates backward, and at troughs they read higher than point-in-time estimates. They are an upper bound for trough multiples.

GLJ's $470 target works out to about 15.6x GLJ's own FY27 EBITDA.

### 3. What the evidence supports and what it does not

**It supports:**
- The Power margin gain is not mix. It is organic in 2026.
- GLJ's vintage figures are changes in a backlog average, not vintage levels. On GEV's own numbers, the 2024 vintage sits above 3 contribution points.
- The RPO schedule places $20.6B of pre-2026 equipment backlog in 2027 revenue.
- Company-level revenue and FCF guidance have never been cut since the spin.
- New-order pricing is still rising at all three turbine makers (TP-30, TP-31, PP-26).

**It does not support:**
- A claim that GLJ's timing is wrong. Management's own statements lean toward GLJ's lag.
- A claim that refuting GLJ closes the gap to consensus. At most about 30% of GLJ's gap is at stake.
- A claim that the vintage idea is new information.
- A claim that the price carries a GLJ discount.
- A claim that customer dockets prove a GE-specific price increase.

**What the evidence says about direction.** The measured gaps point in different directions:
- Consensus is above management's 2028 framework, which reads bearish unless the framework is raised again.
- Management's raise record reads bullish for that raise happening.
- The price requires consensus plus a roughly held multiple. Both the stock's history and the September rate move show that multiple can compress by 25% or more without any estimate cut.

The team's own decision rule has to settle these. None of the tests gives a number on a named line and year that differs from consensus by a clear margin. Tables A-C and F in the direction lookup are where the team places its own number.

**Timing.** No GEV print falls before the finals. Inside the 3-12 month horizon, the dated tests are:
- Q3'26 on 2026-10-28.
- The FY27 outlook in late January 2027, the first under the new CFO, Claire McDonough (PP-32).
- The Capital Markets Day with a 2030 outlook in spring 2027.
- Siemens Energy's medium-term framework on 2026-11-11.

Management says the January margin-in-backlog page mostly informs 2029-30. It is not a 2027 test (PP-06).

### 4. Numbers that were struck, and why

The full list is in "struck". The main items:

- **"$14,087M increase in deposits inside H1'26 OCF."** The number exists, but it is the Note 9 change in *total* contract liabilities, which includes the Prolec acquisition and non-current items (text: $14,088M). The line inside the $10,680M operating cash flow is **$13,695M**. The $10,680M is correct.
- **The team's original vintage margins (7.2 / 11.2 / 20.4).** They used backlog that still included EDF. Corrected: about 5.2-5.9 / 7.5 / 20.4.
- **"Vintage rebuttal restores $0.6-1.3B to GLJ."** This is inconsistent with GLJ's own 2.2-point Power gap. The ceiling is about $0.55-0.62B.
- **"$5.8B cap on the pre-2025 Power book."** It is a floor.
- **"3 of 7 raise days fell."** The Dec-2024 update came after the close, so its reaction day is 12-11 at +5.01%. The correct count is 2 of 7.
- **The management "floor" of $7.8-8.8B for 2027-28 FCF.** It is an artifact of the $24B framework never being restated after the FY26 FCF guide more than doubled.
- **The PEG table across peers.** It mixes EPS bases, growth windows and very thin FY28 estimates.
- **"Chartloom trough multiples are a lower bound."** Wrong sign. They are an upper bound.
- **"Reservation price was reset at conversion" (LG&E/KU).** The reservation was paid in part to lock firm equipment pricing. The overrun is not attributed to GEV.
- **The Q2'26 "adjusted EPS" consensus of $3.04 and the EBITDA consensus of $1.28B.** GEV publishes no adjusted EPS, and the EBITDA figure has no source.
- **Unreachable sources.** The Yahoo, MarketWatch, Barchart and FindBillion figures could not be re-fetched.

### 5. What is still unknown

- GEV's services and equipment margins inside Power. Tests 1-2 cannot close without them.
- The Power-only two-year RPO conversion, and the over-time versus point-in-time revenue split.
- GLJ's full note: its FY27 segment build, anything on FY28, and how it reached $470. About $1.4-1.5B of its gap is unexplained.
- An independent source for the "20.7% consensus Power 2027 margin". It is GLJ's figure only.
- The Vineyard Wind settlement cost, which is confidential, and whether it sits inside the Wind guide.
- Q3 buybacks. The CEO said Q3 "gave another good opportunity", so the $3,037M remaining is stale.
- Whether a December 2026 investor event will be held.
- Consensus FY27 and FY28 FCF have one source (MarketScreener). No free segment consensus exists.
- The LG&E/KU-GE power-island agreement was scanned. Every page is redacted.
