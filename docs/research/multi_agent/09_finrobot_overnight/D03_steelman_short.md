- **What consensus requires:**
  - FY27: Power at 21.7–23.5% when Electrification is at 20.5–22% [computed: D01 Q11, from consensus CT-02 and FY26 guide]. The 2028 Power target is 22% [S1: Q4'25 release -26-000012] [S2: Dec-2025 release src:2711]. Consensus therefore puts Power at its 2028 target a year early.

Spread over a research range of $13–16B of FY28 Power equipment revenue (team's choice; D01 estimates FY27 at ~$12.7B), that is ~6.0–7.4 points of Power equipment margin, i.e. ~55–65% of the >11-point step the CEO places in 2029–30. **Q2. Margin guide misses while revenue and FCF beat: pattern or noise?**

**Q10. The 1999–2002 boom and bust**

- GE shipped more than 60 GW in 2001 [SINGLE-SOURCE: corpus src:296].
- July 2002: GE cut about 2,500 jobs and forecast US gas-turbine orders and shipments down "80 percent or more over the next two years" [S1: GE Power Systems release 2002-07-23].
- Power Systems was 17.3% of GE revenue in 2002 and 13.8% in 2003. Its EPS contribution was +$0.09 in 2002 but −$0.14 in 2003 on the shipment decline [S1: GE 2003 10-K/Annual Report ex13] [S2: GE 2004 10-K revenue-share text].
- **What rhymes:**
  - A demand narrative (then "The Internet Begins with Coal"; now AI).
  - New non-utility buyers placing block reservations.
  - OEM capacity adds.
  - Profits peaked the year *after* orders collapsed, because backlog kept shipping.
- **What does not:**
  - Non-refundable deposits.
  - A much larger services annuity.
  - Regulated-utility IRPs.
  - Supply bottlenecks deeper in the chain.
- **Implication:** an order bust would hit the *multiple* first, not EBITDA, inside 12 months.
