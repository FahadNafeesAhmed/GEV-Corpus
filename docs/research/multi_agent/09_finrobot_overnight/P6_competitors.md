**Normalized earnings estimate.** Start with about $3.1B of operating income. Add modest net interest income on a roughly $9B net cash position. Apply a tax rate of about 21–23%. This gives normalized net income of roughly **$2.5–2.9B**, which implies an underlying **trailing P/E of ~90–100x**, not 27x.

## 5. Scale & Growth Position

- **Scale.** At $41.4B, GEV is at the top of the peer group. It is roughly tied with Siemens Energy and Schneider, and about 25% above the peer median (~$33B).
- **Backlog.** GEV and Siemens Energy both carry record backlogs well above annual revenue. The gas turbine market is effectively sold out into the late 2020s, with slot reservations extending further.
- **Growth drivers.** The main drivers are:
  - data center and AI-driven power demand;
  - coal-to-gas switching;
  - grid build-out, including HVDC and transformers;
  - optionality in small modular nuclear reactors (the BWRX-300).
- **Scale advantage.** In a three-player oligopoly for heavy-duty gas turbines (GEV, Siemens Energy, MHI), scale in manufacturing capacity is itself a competitive weapon. That is because industry capacity additions are deliberately disciplined.
