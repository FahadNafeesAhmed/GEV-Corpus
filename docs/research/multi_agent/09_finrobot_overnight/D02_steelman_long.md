## 2. Answers to your questions

### Q1. Escalation and pass-through in existing contracts

**Finding: mostly no for gas equipment. Yes for services, but at fixed rates.** This does little to soften the "old cheap book" problem on equipment.

**Equipment is fixed-price.**
- The 10-K says: "We enter into contracts that commit to a fixed price well before project completion." It lists "inflation, labor and material cost increases" as a risk. [S1: 10-K -26-000015 Item 1A, offset ~81.8k] [S2: 10-K 0001996810-25-000011 Item 1A (same language; D01 Q7)]
- The word "escalation" appears in the FY25 10-K only in the lease note. "Price adjustment" and "indexed" do not appear at all (searched).
- Billing adjustments cover project delays and performance guarantees only, not inflation [10-K -26-000015 Item 8, revenue estimates].

**Customer evidence points to firm pricing, not indexation.**
- An LG&E/KU reservation of $25M was paid in part "to lock in firm pricing for the equipment" [S1: KY PSC order 2025-10-28, facts VF-03] [S2: evidence brief Test on LG&E/KU].

**Strategy since the spin.**
- The spin filings say GEV is "improving protections embedded in customer agreements to protect against inflation" and is shortening bid cycles. For Wind, they point to "better inflation protection mechanisms". [S1: S-1 0001193125-24-066951] [S2: Form 10 exhibit 8-K 0001193125-24-063739 ex99.1 — same issuer, so weak independence]
- The Q2'26 call says H1'26 onshore Wind "had fewer contractual protections for tariffs since we signed these orders before their implementation". That implies newer orders carry protection [CALL-2Q26, via D01 Q7; **SINGLE-SOURCE** inference].

**Services do escalate, but at a fixed rate.**
- Talen's Guernsey 8-K exhibit describes a 25-year GE CSA signed 2019-06-28: "a monthly fixed fee… and variable fees… per gas turbine fired hour with an annual 2.5% escalation on both fees", plus spare parts "$20,000 plus escalation" [S1: 8-K/EX 0001622536-25-000013 ex99.4, note (c)].
- Company-level corroboration: "margin enhancement potential in our services business due to price escalation" [S2: GEV S-1 0001193125-24-066951].
- A fixed 2.5% escalator ran below 2022–24 cost inflation. That fits the unfavourable CSA profitability revisions: FY24 −$319M ("due primarily to higher costs"), FY25 −$151M, H1'26 −$218M [S1: 10-Q -26-000148 Note 9, offset ~40.1k] [S2: 10-K -26-000015 Note 9 / D01 Q4].

**Net effect.**
- There is no evidence that escalation lifts pre-2025 equipment margins.
- The long argument has to rest on dollar share: pre-2025 orders are only 8–20% of 2027 equipment revenue [brief §5; vintage model]. Escalation adds nothing to it.
- On services, a fixed ~2.5% escalator turns from a drag to neutral if cost inflation settles near 2.5–3%. That is a research inference and is not quantifiable from disclosures.
