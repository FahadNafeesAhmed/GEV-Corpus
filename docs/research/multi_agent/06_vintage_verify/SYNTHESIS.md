# Adjudication — the backlog-vintage argument

## 1. Verdict

**The argument is correct, is genuinely unaddressed by GLJ, and should not be the memo's lead sentence.**

One reason dominates: **we cut the cheap cohort on the wrong date.** We used the 31-Dec-2024 book because GLJ says 2027 ships a 2024-priced order book. But no dated GEV statement anywhere puts a price step-up at the 2024/2025 boundary. Management's own price comparator is the **31-Dec-2025 backlog**. The "+10-20 points" figure first appears on the Q4'25 call (2026-01-28) measured against the book *as it then stood*; it was restated on 2026-04-22 against Q4'25 orders and raised to ">20%" on 2026-07-22. In April 2025 management was still describing pricing as cost mitigation "including use of existing contractual provisions." On 2025-12-09 it still listed "pricing on gas turbines above current orders pricing levels" as an *opportunity to our outlook* — i.e. not in the plan. And it dated the revenue arrival itself: the "new price paradigm orders start to come into revenue" in **H2'26 and beyond** (Q3'25 call, 2025-10-22).

So 9-19% answers a question about a vintage boundary management never drew. A judge who covers this name will draw the real boundary and ask what happens. The good news: **restating on management's own boundary makes the rebuttal stronger, not weaker.** That restatement is the exhibit. The lead should be a disclosed margin fact that needs no allocation at all.

## 2. The corrected number

Pre-2025-vintage share of 2027 equipment revenue:

| basis | share |
|---|---|
| Blended, as-disclosed percentages | **8% – 20%** (central ~12%) |
| Blended, allowing measured lengthening (2-yr realisation 65% not 69%) | **8% – 23%** |
| **Power segment only** — what GLJ's gas premise actually requires | **6% – 16%** |

Arithmetic, blended: $43,047M × (93% − 69%) = **$10,331M** available for 2027-2029. 2027's slice across back-loaded (25%) to front-loaded (55%) splits = **$2,583M – $5,682M**. Denominator: consensus FY27 revenue $52,740M × equipment 55-60% = $29,007M – $31,644M. Range = 8.2% to 19.6%.

Power only: $12,461M × 24% = $2,991M → 2027 slice $748M – $1,645M, against 2027 Power equipment revenue of $10.4-12.4B = 6.0% to 15.9%.

**The reframe that should carry the memo.** Cut at 31-Dec-2025 instead — management's own boundary — and no interpolation is needed at all, because that cohort's disclosed 2-year window ends exactly 31-Dec-2027. $64,245M × (69% − 37%) = **$20,558M lands in calendar 2027**. Subtract the pre-2025 slice and 2027 equipment revenue decomposes three ways:

| contract vintage | $M | share of 2027 equipment revenue |
|---|---|---|
| signed before 2025 | 2,583 – 5,682 | **8 – 20%** |
| signed during 2025 | 14,876 – 17,976 | **47 – 62%** |
| signed 2026 or later | 8,449 – 11,086 | 29 – 35% |

**GLJ prices the 2025 vintage at 10-11 points itself.** So on GEV's own disclosure, the vintage that actually supplies half of 2027 is the one GLJ calls good. GLJ needs 2027 to be 2024-vintage to get 3 points; the disclosure says it is not.

Remaining assumptions: (a) 2027's slice of the single 3-5 year bucket is bounded, never stated; (b) the runoff curve is blended across gas, wind and grid — no segment version exists; (c) equipment is 55-60% of 2027 revenue (it was 55.0% in FY25, 57.3% in H1'26); (d) the 2024 cohort's *actual* 2-year realisation is unobservable, so 69% is management's estimate, not a fact.

## 3. What broke

Five things. Three are real damage.

1. **Wrong cohort date** (above). The biggest break. Fix by moving to the 31-Dec-2025 cut and the three-vintage table.
2. **Wrong pool.** The $43,047M book was only **28.9% Power** ($12,461M) — Wind $10,720M, Electrification $20,005M. We rebutted a gas-pricing thesis with a pool that is 71% transformers and wind turbines. GEV *does* disclose equipment RPO by segment, every quarter, in MD&A. Restating Power-only gives 6-16%, so the blend was flattering GLJ, not us — but only if we show it segment-wise.
3. **One curve applied to unlike pools.** Measured 2025 conversion of each segment's opening equipment book: Power 53.7%, Wind 67.6%, Electrification 36.9% — a 1.85x spread against the blended 44%. There is no segment runoff disclosure, so the Power restatement has to borrow the blended curve's *shape*. Label it an inference.
4. **The upper bound is not robust.** The 1-year share fell 44% → 37% → 36% and the 2-year 69% → **65%** in Q2'26 — the largest single-quarter move in the series, pushing $3.5B past H1'28. If the 2024 cohort's true 2-year realisation was 65%, the answer is 9.5-22.9%. This is why the top of the range must be 22-23%, not 19%.
5. **Denominator was too low.** The 30-Jun-2026 book *alone* supplies $28.6-29.2B of 2027 equipment revenue before a single new order — 54-55% of total consensus revenue. New intake can only add, so equipment is at or above the top of the 55-60% band. This pushes the share *down* about a point.

Two attacks **failed** and we can say so: Prolec and held-for-sale do not corrupt the 2024 cohort (Prolec was a 50% equity-method investment at both year-ends; acquisition contribution to FY24 and FY25 equipment revenue is disclosed as zero). Cancellations cannot speed the runoff, because cancellable orders are excluded from RPO by definition.

**Weakest remaining link:** the over-time vs point-in-time revenue split is disclosed nowhere — not in either 10-K, all eight 10-Qs, the five 424B3s, the Form 10 information statement, or the FY25 XBRL definition linkbase (no `RevenueRecognitionTimingAxis`). For the point-in-time slice, GLJ's shipment-year logic is the *correct* mechanic, not ours. Stress-tested: breaking 19% requires the point-in-time share of the 2027-2029 pool to exceed 40% **and** 83% of those dollars to land in 2027 specifically. Onshore wind, the largest point-in-time candidate, runs a ~12-month order-to-delivery cycle, so it is largely absent from a 2024 cohort's year-3-5 residue. The hole is real but it does not break the number.

## 4. What the memo should lead with

Lead with the **disclosed margin realisation**, not the allocation. Power segment EBITDA margin: 9.88% (FY23) → 12.51% (FY24) → 14.68% (FY25) → 17.63% (H1'26), 18.82% in Q2'26, with FY2026 guidance of **17-19%** and a 2028 target of **22%** — and the Power services share of segment revenue *fell* in FY25, so the improvement happened against a mix headwind. On our own cohort model, FY25 and FY26 revenue *is* substantially the pre-2025 book. The vintage GLJ calls a 3-point book is the vintage already shipping at 17-19% segment EBITDA. No allocation assumption, no undisclosed bucket, management's own guidance. Then bring the three-vintage table as exhibit 2.

## 5. Things to fix before anything is written

Do not print "margin in backlog" as a margin forecast. The implied old-book figure is ~19.56% and reported FY2025 equipment gross margin was **10.39%** (13.43% ex the disclosed $637M Offshore Wind charge) — a **9-point** unexplained gap. The phrase appears in neither 10-K and is never defined. Present the 6 points of accretion as management's own characterisation and direction of travel only.

Also: GLJ is not a margin-collapse thesis. Its $7.42B FY27 adj EBITDA is 14.07% of consensus revenue against GEV's FY25 *actual* 8.40%. It embeds 567bp of expansion. Attacking it as a collapse call will not survive a judge who has read the note. Attack the *vintage attribution*, which is where it is wrong.

## The three hardest judge challenges

CHALLENGE 1 — "Your runoff curve is blended across gas, wind and grid. GLJ's argument is about gas turbines. Why should I believe a number that is 71% transformers and wind?"
ANSWER: We do not rely on the blend, and the blend was flattering GLJ, not us. GEV discloses equipment RPO by segment every quarter in MD&A. At 31-Dec-2024 the $43,047M equipment book was Power $12,461M (28.9%), Wind $10,720M (24.8%), Electrification $20,005M (46.3%) — stated verbatim in two separate filings: FY2024 10-K (0001996810-25-000011) MD&A pp.38/39/40, "RPO December 31 2024 2023 2022 Equipment $12,461 $13,636 $13,579", and re-disclosed in the FY2025 10-K (0001996810-26-000015) pp.26/28/30. Restated Power-only, the pre-2025 share of 2027 Power equipment revenue is 6%-16%, LOWER than the blend, because Electrification — 46.3% of the book — is the slowest converter (36.9% one-year realised conversion in 2025 versus Power 53.7%). The slow tail of the blended curve is grid, not gas. Independent physical cross-check in gigawatts: end-2024 gas backlog was ~25 GW (derived two ways that agree to 0.5 GW) against 2025 actual plus 2026E gas shipments of 29.3 GW — 117% coverage, so the 2024 gas book is physically exhausted before 2027 reaches the shop floor.
EXHIBIT: segment equipment RPO table, three columns (Power / Wind / Electrification) at 31-Dec-2024, with the Power-only restatement beneath it and the GW roll-forward as a footnote.

CHALLENGE 2 — "You picked 31-Dec-2024 as the price boundary. Management's own price statements are all benchmarked to the end-2025 backlog. Isn't your cohort a full year too early?"
ANSWER: Yes, and correcting it strengthens the argument. No dated management statement anywhere puts a price step-up at the 2024/2025 boundary; we searched all ten earnings releases 1Q'24-2Q'26, both 10-Ks, all eight 10-Qs, the Dec-2025 investor deck and every pre-2026 dated source in our corpus. The "+10-20 points" figure originates on the Q4'25 call, 2026-01-28, Strazik verbatim: "When we look at where we're trending with our slot reservation agreements today versus our existing backlog, there's another 10 to 20 points of pricing strength in the SRAs today." It was restated on 2026-04-22 against Q4'25 orders and raised to ">20%" on 2026-07-22. On 2025-12-09 the investor deck still listed "Pricing on gas turbines above current orders pricing levels" under "Opportunities to our outlook" — not in the base case. So we cut at 31-Dec-2025, which needs no interpolation at all because that cohort's disclosed two-year window ends exactly 31-Dec-2027: $64,245M x (69%-37%) = $20,558M in calendar 2027. Decomposed, 2027 equipment revenue is 8-20% pre-2025 vintage, 47-62% 2025 vintage, 29-35% 2026-or-later. GLJ itself prices the 2025 vintage at 10-11 points. So on GEV's own disclosure, the vintage that supplies roughly half of 2027 is the vintage GLJ calls good, and GLJ's 3-point assumption is applied to a cohort that supplies at most a fifth of the year.
EXHIBIT: the three-vintage stacked bar of 2027 equipment revenue, with the two RPO notes cited beneath it.

CHALLENGE 3 — "Margin in backlog is not GAAP, is not defined, and appears in neither 10-K. Your implied 19.56% old-book margin is contradicted by the 10.39% equipment gross margin GEV actually reported for FY2025. Which one is real?"
ANSWER: Both are as reported, and GEV does not reconcile them — we will not claim it does. Reported FY2025 equipment gross margin was 10.39% ($20,934M revenue less $18,759M cost, FY2025 10-K p.38); adding back the disclosed $637M Offshore Wind incremental contract loss gives 13.43%. The implied book margin at 31-Dec-2024, solved from the Q4'25 release ("grew equipment margin in backlog by $8B, with 6 pts of accretion") against equipment RPO of $43,047M and $64,245M, is 19.56% rising to 25.56%. That is a roughly 9-point gap, partly Offshore Wind loss contracts and Wind's negative margin, partly a definitional difference that GEV never states — the phrase "margin in backlog" appears in neither 10-K and lives only in earnings press releases. We therefore use the 6 points of accretion only as management's own characterisation and direction of travel, never as a recognised-margin forecast, and we forecast margin off the disclosed segment EBITDA path instead: Power 9.88% (FY23) to 12.51% (FY24) to 14.68% (FY25) to 17.63% (H1'26), FY2026 guidance 17-19%, 2028 target 22%. That path needs no undefined metric and no allocation. A second reason to prefer it: in FY2025 the Power services share of segment revenue FELL from 68.4% to 66.1%, so the margin improvement happened against a mix headwind toward lower-margin equipment.
