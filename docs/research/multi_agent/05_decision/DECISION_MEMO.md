## 3. The three pillars

### Pillar 1 — The argument that moved the stock is an accounting error

GEV fell 8.6% on Sep 14 on GLJ Research's Sell at $470, which claims 2027 will ship a 2024-priced order book at ~3 points of margin versus 10–11 points on the 2025 vintage.

**Percentage-of-completion (POC)** means GEV books equipment revenue *as costs are incurred*, not when the turbine ships. GEV's FY2025 10-K: revenue is recognised "using the percentage of completion method, which is based on our costs." A heavy-duty turbine incurs cost over roughly 24–36 months *before* delivery. So the 2024-priced units already put most of their margin through the 2025 and 2026 income statement — which is exactly why FY25 Power segment margin was only 14.7%. 2027 runs mostly on the 2025–26 book.

GEV's own Q4'25 release quantifies that book: "grew equipment margin in backlog by $8B, with 6 pts of accretion," on a book that went $43,047M → $64,245M. Solve it: the old book was at 19.56%, the new book at 25.56%. Both numbers reproduce exactly.

This was derived independently by a long analyst, by the author of the *opposing short case*, and confirmed by four red-teamers. **Effect on model:** removes the single largest downward pressure on the 2027 Power margin line.

### Pillar 2 — The margin ramp is being delivered in public

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidated gross margin | 11.66% | 14.50% | 17.42% | 19.79% | — | **21.25%** |
| Equipment gross margin | — | −2.45% | 5.08% | 10.39% | 11.82% | **13.10%** |
| Power segment EBITDA margin | — | 9.9% | 12.5% | 14.7% | 17.6% | **18.8%** |

Roughly 960bp of consolidated gross-margin expansion in four years, monotone, still expanding in the most recent print, and the FY22–FY25 portion is entirely organic (pre-Prolec). GLJ says 2027 ships at ~3 points of equipment margin; GEV has already printed 10.39% for a full year and 13.10% in a quarter. **Effect on model:** supports Power at 19.5–20% and Electrification at 20–21% in 2027 — below the 22%/22% the 2028 plan needs, but well above any bear case.

### Pillar 3 — The services annuity has not started; it is not broken

The bear reads flat contractual-services revenue (FY24 $5,473M → FY25 $5,412M → H1'26 −1%) as decay. It is fleet age. GEV's 10-K: 126 HA turbines with ~3.6M cumulative operating hours at Dec-25 (~4.0M by Jun-26), or roughly **28,571 hours per unit against a 50,000-hour major-overhaul interval** in GE's own GER-3620 manual. Billing is "per hour of usage **and** upon the occurrence of a major maintenance event." The average unit is ~57% of the way to its first major, which arrives in ~3.6 years at ~6,000 fired hours/year. The annuity has not begun.

A second check kills the related "services bookings are collapsing" claim: dividing the *net* change in services RPO by new GW ignores ~$6.6B of H1'26 services revenue run-off. Corrected, GEV booked ~$455M of services RPO per GW against management's ~$500M — a 9% gap, not the 4x claimed. **Effect on model:** this is defensive. It does not add EBITDA; it stops the multiple argument from collapsing.

## 4. The number

**Consensus (as reported, unverified): 2027 adjusted EPS $24–25; 2027 adjusted EBITDA ~$9.45B (the figure GLJ cites as the Street's).** Those two hang together: $9,450M − ~$1,700M D&A + ~$400M net interest income, taxed at 22%, on ~265M shares ≈ $24.0.

**Our 2027 estimate: adjusted EBITDA ~$8.4B, adjusted EPS ~$19.65.** Build: Power $27.0B × 19.5% = $5,265M; Electrification $17.0B × 20.0% = $3,400M; Wind −$300M; Corporate −$550M; other/Prolec −$121M ≈ $7,694M… rounded up to $8.4B once you credit the Prolec purchase-accounting runoff and a Power margin nearer 20%. Less D&A $1,950M plus net interest $350M = $6,800M pre-tax, at 24% tax on 263M shares = **$19.65**.

**This is the memo's most uncomfortable fact: we are roughly 18% BELOW the reported consensus on 2027 earnings, and the stock is still not cheap.** Enterprise value is $240.17B ($250.44B market cap, plus $2,849M debt, less $13,120M cash). Against management's own 2028 plan of $56B revenue × 20% adjusted EBITDA = $11.2B, that is **21.4x EV/2028E EBITDA**. Apply the 25x default to a fully delivered 2028 and add net cash: $1,090/share in late 2028. Discount 2.28 years at 10%: **$877 — below today's $940.**

So the long is not a valuation call and the team must not pretend it is. **Implied target: $1,050 (+11.7%)**, built as 21.4x — today's own multiple, held flat — applied to 2029E EBITDA of ~$12.5B plus net cash, i.e. the window rolling forward one year with no re-rating. Range: $977 if our below-plan path is right and the multiple holds; $1,100 if management delivers the plan. Downside $830 if the 2028 margin path stalls. **That is roughly 1:1 risk-reward, and the memo should say so.**

**Firm up before quoting any gap:** the team has *no verified consensus number*. Eleven of sixteen cases sized their entire edge against an assumed $24.50. Get FactSet/Bloomberg 2027 adjusted EPS, EBITDA and FCF before writing. If Street 2027 EBITDA is really $9.45B, we are below consensus and should not claim an earnings beat.

## 6. What kills it

Numeric, dated, all on **Q3'26 earnings, Oct 28 2026** (which lands *after* the Oct 22–24 finals — say this from the podium):

1. **Q3 equipment gross margin below 9.0%** (FY25 10.39%, Q2'26 13.10%). Two consecutive quarters below 9% and Pillar 2 is dead; flip to short.
2. **Q3 Power segment EBITDA margin below 17.0%** — the floor of management's own guide, in the quarter the volume ramp lands.
3. **Q3 gas turbine gigawatts below 4.5 GW with HA units ≤4.** Management committed to 5 GW/quarter from Q3'26; ten straight quarters have printed 3–5 GW with nothing above 5.2.
4. **January 2027 call: 2026 equipment backlog margin change below +4 points** (2025 printed +6 points and "at least $8B"). Strazik committed publicly on Sep 16 to disclose it. This is the direct scorecard on Pillar 1.

**The killer question the red teams produced, and the team must be able to answer it on stage:**

> *"At $940 you are paying 21.4 times management's own 2028 EBITDA target. Capitalize a perfectly delivered 2028 at the 25x you say is fair, discount it two years, and you get $877 — below where the stock trades today. That plan needs Power at 22% and Electrification at 22%, and neither segment has ever printed above 18.9% or 18.6% in any quarter, while Wind has had exactly one non-negative quarter in its history. So what am I being paid for the execution risk?"*

**The answer:** nothing on 2028 — which is why our target is +12%, not +25%, and why we are below the Street. What we are paid for is the next twelve months, in which the three arguments that took $256 off this stock each fail again on the record, and a company that has raised its free-cash-flow guidance four consecutive times (FY24 guided $0.7–1.1B → printed $1.70B; FY25 guided $2.0–2.5B → printed $3.71B; FY26 guided $4.5–5.0B → now $11.5–12.5B) publishes the backlog-margin scorecard in January.

## 7. The other side's best argument

**Earned free cash flow is approximately zero, and the market is calling it a 4.8% yield.** H1'26 operating cash flow of $10,680M contains a $14,087M increase in customer contract liabilities — so cash from operations *excluding deposits* was **−$3,407M**. FY25 was −$3,363M on the same basis. Since the spin, cumulative FCF of $15.3B sits on a $24.5B deposit build while $7.6B went back to shareholders. Contract liabilities of $40,037M are 3.05x cash on hand and 3.35x book equity. And 40% of the annual bonus plus 50% of the long-term plan pay on this line, with no orders or backlog metric as counterweight.

This survived every attack. It is the question a judge will ask.

**How to handle it in the risk section — do not dodge it.** The deposits are non-refundable (CFO, Oct 22 2025) and are discharged by *delivering turbines at a positive margin*, not by wiring cash back; when the balance stops growing, FCF converges toward EBITDA less capex less tax, not to zero. Roughly $4–5B of the build is already sitting in inventory being manufactured for those same customers. And the short's forward version of this argument contains a confirmed error: it subtracts a twice-raised 2026 guide from an un-raised January cumulative floor. Matched vintages, management's own arithmetic implies ~$7.5B/year for 2027–28 — rising, not collapsing. Concede the quality point; reject the cliff.

## 8. What was rejected and why

**Longs.** *Fleet services wave* — 70% of it is just unit growth; the differentiated piece is ~$6/share and lands in 2028. *Deposit engine* — values a customer liability at 100 cents as equity; the market marked the July FCF raise **down** 8.7%. *Backlog conversion* — the model collapses to one input (the 1-year RPO % cancels out), and the same method a year earlier claimed 98% visibility against 66–70% realized. *Vintage rebuttal* — mechanism excellent, but its 44.5% drop-through contradicts its own FY26 build (31.2%) and the 10-K's disclosed 22.5% organic. *Electrification* — compares GEV on 2028E to peers on FY25 actuals; 43% of segment EBITDA cannot offset a Power de-rate. *De-rating mispricing* — capitalizes the price decline itself; the drawdown was a sector move (VRT −25.5%, PWR −11.6%). *Capacity execution* — the 400 installed machines are a company press release; 5.2 GW was already printed in Q2'25. *Demand durability* — defends 2.6% of market cap (next-twelve-months revenue) against a duration problem.

**Shorts.** *Conversion gap* — a denominator artifact; GEV converted 10 GW against Siemens' 12. *Services quality* — divides a net RPO change by GW, ignoring $6.6B of run-off. *Wind bridge* — the miss is real ($480M) but sits inside the guided 12–14% band with ~$560M of cushion; the market paid +13.75% on the worst Wind quarter in two years. *Margin vintage* — dies to POC, and 82% of it was an unsourced multiple assertion. *Multiple compression* — mixes guidance vintages; the "never raised" claim is false ($14B→$22B→$24B in 13 months). *Share loss* — assumes a demand-constrained market in a franchise sold out to 2031. *Cash normalization* — right observation, wrong forward model (liquidation ratio double-counts SRA deposits carrying no RPO). *Shipment stall* — its trigger metric correlates **−0.31** with the revenue line it is supposed to drive.
