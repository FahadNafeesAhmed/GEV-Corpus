## 4. Credit and suppliers

**Credit says: no stress, and mildly improving.** GEV issued $2.6B of notes on Feb 2 2026 at +45/+65/+78bp. Today the 2036 note trades at ~63bp. Over the same window the ICE BofA Single-A index OAS widened from 60bp to 65bp — **GEV tightened 2bp while its cohort widened 5bp, outperforming by ~7bp**, across both a +26% rally and a −19% drawdown. Fitch upgraded to A− on Aug 12/13 2026 citing ~1x gross leverage and $13.1B liquidity, a month before GLJ's Sell. GEV trades ~32bp inside the BBB index despite S&P still rating it BBB.

State the limit plainly: at ~1x leverage with $10.3B net cash, bondholders would be largely indifferent to a severe earnings miss. Flat spreads are strong evidence against a *solvency* bear case and no evidence at all about the multiple.

**Suppliers corroborate the ramp and cap the upside.** Howmet — >50% global share of industrial gas turbine blades — grew IGT revenue 38% to $341M in Q2'26 and says every new gigawatt-scale increment lands in **2028, 2029, or 2029–2030**, and that machine tools ordered today deliver "earliest August of '28." OEMs are emailing for 2027–28 volume it cannot supply. Woodward's power-generation sales rose 19% with customers raising forecasts into the early 2030s. Siemens Energy booked 15 GW against 6 GW shipped. Nobody flagged a slowdown as of Sep 9.

So 2026–27 output is bounded by capacity already installed. **Upside inside the horizon should come from price and services mix, not from volume above the published ladder.** Separately, GE Aerospace agreed on Sep 8 to buy castings house CPP for $11.75B (closing H2 2027) — aerospace and power now openly compete for the same hot-section capacity.

## 10. What changed versus the team's current view

**Strengthens the long.** Credit outperformed its own rating cohort by ~7bp through both the rally and the drawdown, and Fitch upgraded a month before GLJ's Sell. The drawdown was not forced selling and was not GEV-specific — one earnings miss plus a sector AI-capex scare. EPA removed the CCS requirement on Sep 14. Suppliers confirm the physical ramp is real. Vineyard Wind removed an >$800M claim. Management is buying stock.

**Weakens it.** Shipments are *falling* year over year — 5 GW in Q3'26 would be the largest quarter since separation off a declining base, so the Oct 28 bar is harder than "the ramp is underway." Consensus FY26 EBITDA already sits at the *top* of a guided band management pointedly did not raise. Consensus is 8–16% above GEV's own 2028 targets, so an in-line December reaffirmation is a negative. And H1 prepayments exceeded operating cash flow outright.

**Reframes it.** The reservation-conversion worry is real but mis-dated: Strazik said Q4 commitments will exceed Q3, so the "20 GW is conservative" upside lands in *January*, not October. Three corrections change memo text: Power organic guidance is 18–20% (not 16–18%), the Prolec gain is $3,992M (not $4.4B), and the S&P forward multiple is ~19x (not 22x), which makes GEV's premium larger than assumed.

**My lean: 52/48 long, weaker than the team's 55/45** — because the only cheap-looking version of the valuation depends on treating deposit-funded FY26 FCF as a run-rate, and consensus itself does not.
