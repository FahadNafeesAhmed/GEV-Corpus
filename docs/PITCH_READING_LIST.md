For the team. Everything here is public. Read the examples for **structure and rhythm only** — the ideas
are irrelevant; the skeleton is what we're copying.

## 1. What an industry-standard 2-page memo looks like

This is the shape of what a buy-side analyst sends a portfolio manager. Proportions matter as much as content.

| Section | Length | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Header** | 3 lines | Ticker · LONG/SHORT · price → target (±%) · horizon · catalyst date. Judges read this first. |
| **Thesis** | 3–4 sentences | What the market believes, why it's wrong, what happens, when. The whole trade in ~80 words. |
| **What's priced in** | 1 paragraph | The Street's growth, margin and multiple assumptions — from guidance, attributed analyst calls, and a reverse-DCF. Most student memos skip this. We won't. |
| **Variant view** | **~40% of the page** | 2–3 points. Each: bolded claim → sourced evidence → what it does to the numbers. |
| **Valuation** | 1 short paragraph + 1 small table | Method, key drivers, base/bull/bear with probabilities → target. |
| **Catalysts** | 3 dated bullets | What makes the market find out, inside 3–12 months. |
| **Risks & what proves us wrong** | 3 bullets | Real risks with mitigants — and the kill condition as **a number and a date**. |
| **Exhibit** | 1 chart | The one picture that makes the argument. Counts toward the two pages. |

### Style rules that signal "professional"

- **Every number has a source** in parentheses: *(10-Q Q2'26, note 12)*. Every one.
- **No adjective without a number.** "Strong backlog" → "$176B RPO, ~4.5 years at the current 3 GW/quarter shipment rate."
- **Bolded lead sentence** on every paragraph. A reader should get the argument from the bold text alone.
- Short sentences. Active voice. "We," not "I."
- **No hedging words.** "Could," "might," "may" become probabilities or get cut.
- The kill condition is specific: *"If Q3 shipments exceed X GW on Oct 22, we are wrong."*
- "Cheap" is not a catalyst. If we lead with valuation, the first question is "why hasn't it worked already, and what changes now?"

## 5. How to read them

With a highlighter, for **four things only**:

1. Where the thesis sentence sits, and how long it is.
2. The one sentence that states what's priced in.
3. How every number is sourced.
4. How risks and kill conditions are phrased.

Ignore the ideas. Steal the rhythm.

**Three things to know:**
- **No past HFAC/Citadel winning memo is public.** There is no exact target; the closest proxies are #1 (same level) and #3–5 (the format the judges use daily).
- **The competition examples are decks; ours is two pages.** A 40-slide deck compressed to two pages *is* the memo. Read decks for argument order, buy-side memos for sentence density.
- VIC needs a free guest account (45-day delay on ideas). Some YAVB podcast pages are paywalled past the deck link.

## 7. Rules that bind the submission

- **Memo and model must be blinded**: no university, club, or member names.
- **The official rules require disclosure of AI-assisted initial research and prohibit AI-generated submission content.** The corpus, the cards, and this reading list are research inputs. The sentences in the memo and the forecast numbers in the model are the team's own work.
- One person submits for the whole team.

*Corpus tools for evidence: `python src/ask.py search "..."`, `python src/ask.py comps`, `python src/ask.py backlog`, `python src/ask.py generators`, `python src/embed.py --search "..." --all -k 10`. See `README.md`.*
